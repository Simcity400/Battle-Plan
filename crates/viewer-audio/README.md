# viewer-audio

Map audio for the desktop app's native maps: the game's sound effects for the entity events the map presents and the game's music player, mixed off the frame thread. Definitions and samples come from [installed-content-sound](../installed-content-sound/README.md) through the recording's content stack; the [viewer](../viewer/README.md) supplies each presented frame's events, camera, volumes and song chances.

## Layout

| Area | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| [engine.rs](src/engine.rs) | Device-free instances, voices, falloff, balance, category volumes, compressors and the mixer. |
| [music.rs](src/music.rs) | The music player: weighted song draws, the recently-played history, streamed tracks. |
| [sdl.rs](src/sdl.rs) | The SDL 2.0.20 conversions the game mixes through: the loader's conversion of samples to the mixer format and the device stream's conversion to the device's rate, channels and format (zlib licence, [notice](../../LICENSES/SDL-LICENSE.txt)). |
| [battle.rs](src/battle.rs) | Battle ambience: the recorded battles and the distant ambience and `combat_sounds` layers each audible battle plays. |
| [system.rs](src/system.rs) | One process [`System`](src/system.rs): ownership, the control thread, the sound and music loader threads and the output device. |
| [lib.rs](src/lib.rs) | Frame inputs: emitters, listener, volumes, song chances, battles. |

## What plays

Every entity event with `sound = { soundeffect = ... }` that a model pass presents (units, aircraft, trains, buildings, sprawl, nuclear impacts and the ambient map objects) and every weather entity the map shows owns one instance while the event is present; a weather entity's sound event fires its `time` after the entity first appears and lasts while its region keeps its weather. An ambient map object with `use_animation = no` requests no state, but the game creates every entity instance in its entity's `default_state` and only then, for `use_animation = yes`, requests `idle`; so the default state's animation and sound events play (the vanilla ambient wind and water entities are such objects). The map frame plays that state on the object's own player from when the object appears ([viewer-world](../viewer-world/README.md)), so event times, `trigger_once` and later states follow the state's own rules, and a state with one variant is entered whatever its chance, as the game's state setter draws only among several. An object that can reach a state with several variants, on its root or an attached entity, stays still and silent and is refused by name and counted: the game draws the variant from a random counter that every earlier draw for that entity has advanced since the session began, which no recording holds. The scene lists events by their exact presentation key; a new key or a falling age is a firing. An event first seen more than 0.1 s after it fired (an entity recreated mid-state) keeps only its loop, so recreated entities do not replay what already happened.

The instance follows the game's update each frame, after every voice's fade has moved by the frame's time:

- Falloff: the vertical offset is scaled by `height_scale` when positive; `t = (d - min) / (max - min)` clamped to [0, 1] gives `1 - t` (linear) or `-log10(0.9 t + 0.1)` (logarithmic). Beyond twice the maximum distance the instance's voices stop (with its fade-out) and a loop starts again when it returns. An effect without a falloff is not attenuated.
- `is3d` effects take a linear balance from the listener's right axis against the direction to the emitter; others play centred.
- An instance has one slot per `polyphony` voice when the effect has at least that many sounds, else one. A fired event, or a loop with a free slot, starts the instance: every free slot draws, from the game's audio counter hash, a weighted sound among those no other slot plays (and, with `prevent_random_repetition`, none a slot played last; with `random_sound_when_looping = no` a slot keeps the sound it had), then a delay of the draw times the range's width and a playback-rate offset, each on a fired start or where the `looping_*` switch says so. A weighted draw over no positive weight picks uniformly. In range the slot then creates a voice and draws its volume offset, added to the effect volume and clamped to [0, 1]; out of range a fired start draws but creates no voice. A loop without per-restart draws loops in the mixer by itself.
- `max_audible`: an instance starting with no voice is over the limit when the effect has that many counted instances. Under `fail` it then creates no voice; under `silent` it plays held silent, gives its place up when out of earshot and takes a free place back when in earshot. An instance counts while it holds a voice.
- Fades move the voice's volume by the frame's time over the fade time, so a voice at volume 0.5 is in after half its `fade_in`. A delayed voice waits at its full volume and starts without its fade-in.
- Voices take the mixer's 256 slots, the first eight its own (music); a slot creates no voice when none is free.

The mixer renders 1,024 frames per device callback in the game's 16-bit integer arithmetic:

- A voice's gain is the category volume (the `NSound.HEIGHT_SOUND_CATEGORY` category follows camera altitude between its defines times the ambient volume; the `NSound.VOICE_OVER_CATEGORY` category takes the voice volume), times master and the game's `dev_master_volume`, times the Units or Combat group (both the game's effects volume by default), its falloff gain and its volume (effect and sound volumes and fade), rounded to steps of 1/16384 and split by its balance. It ramps from the last callback's step to this one's across the callback; a voice's first callback has no ramp.
- Only 32 voices mix per callback: music first, then the effect voices with the highest sound `priority` times falloff gain, ties to the later slot. The rest, and voices without a gain to ramp down from, advance a callback at their natural rate in silence.
- At its natural rate a voice adds its samples frame for frame; at another rate the mixer steps a float position from the voice's place, interpolating linearly, and keeps only the whole frames passed at the end of each callback.
- Voices of a category with an enabled compressor, and music when the content has a music compressor, mix into their own buffer, which is compressed only for callbacks in which a voice within the 32 mixed into it: detection after the pregain, one-pole attack and release at `exp(-1 / (44100 t))`, gain reduction `(threshold - level dB) (1 - 1 / ratio)` capped at 0 dB, then the postgain. A repeated category takes its later `compressor` block. Every buffer then joins the sum, music included, which passes the master compressor and is clamped to 16 bits.
- Samples load as the game's loader converts them with SDL 2.0.20: 16-bit stereo at 44,100 Hz unchanged, anything else through float, mono duplicated, resampled by SDL's bandlimited resampler and converted back as SDL's SSE2 path does. The device is opened as the game opens it, at the mixer's own format, and each callback converts to the device's rate, channels and format through SDL's audio stream: each callback resampled on its own with the stream's padding, up-mixed to quad, 5.1 or 7.1 as SDL does, mono mixed down first.

The Combat group is a viewer split: events of actors the producer presents in attack or defend states, strike impacts and the battle ambience. The game has one effects volume.

## Battle ambience

The battles are the game's own battle list, recorded each hour as `BattleSounds` ([battle.rs](src/battle.rs)): each battle's centre, radius and member provinces, in the order the game's per-frame pass visits them. The game changes the list only where it adds and removes combats, so the hour's list is what its frames sound. The list is the recording client's: when two battles merge, the game keeps the one sounding for that client, which depends on its camera. Each battle counts the units of its member provinces' land combats (border wars included), attackers and defenders, summed over each member, and the sub-units of those that are divisions: a side can hold another unit kind (the game's save loader keeps any saved unit reference), which counts as a unit but has no template. A battle's sounds belong to its recorded object, as the game keeps them on the battle: a battle that survives a merge or keeps its place in a regrouping plays on, and one the game creates afresh starts its sounds anew. A recording without `BattleSounds` names the table in the diagnostics and plays no battle ambience.

Each frame, a battle plays when the camera's eye lies within its radius plus the hearing distance (the falloff maximum of the `NSound.BATTLE_SOUND_NAME` effect, or `BATTLE_SOUND_FALLOFF_DEFAULT` when it has none) and its combats hold at least `BATTLE_SOUND_MIN_UNIT_COUNT` units, attackers and defenders. It plays the distant ambience at its centre, 9.5 above the map, and, for each sub-unit type the `sound/combat_sounds` definitions list (in the order they first list it), each definition whose `divisions_range` holds the battle's count of that type: one per regiment or support slot of each division's template, from its recorded grids (regimental-support slots do not count; a division without a template has none). A sound that ends starts again while its battle stays audible. Without the distant effect nothing plays, as the game then skips the whole update; the map's warnings name the missing effect (or unreadable defines). Missing recorded tables are named: without template compositions a battle still plays its distant ambience but no layer, and divisions whose template grids are unrecorded or were unreadable are counted in the refusals and add nothing to the layers.

## Music

The music player plays one track at a time from the songs of the stations the user enabled (`playlists/settings.txt`; every station when the file is absent). Each presented hour, the viewer evaluates every enabled song's `chance` body for the viewing country against the recording: a mean-time weight, its base `factor` times the `factor` and plus the `add` of each modifier whose conditions hold. A song whose conditions the recording cannot answer keeps its base chance, named in the diagnostics. When no track plays, the player draws the next song as the game's player does, with its own counter hash: each song weighs its chance, held as the game's fixed point, times its place in the recently-played history over `NGame.MUSIC_PLAYER_RECENTLY_PLAYED_SIZE` (the song played last weighs only the least weight, 0.001; a song outside the history weighs its whole chance), rounded to units of 1/100000. The drawn song joins the front of the history, which keeps that many songs. When a track ends, the callback it ends in draws the next song, the control thread opens it and waits for its first samples before mixing on (as the game's player opens its next track at once), and the next song starts in the following callback; the rest of the ending callback is silent, as in the game. There is no fade. Tracks are Ogg Vorbis and stream: their own loader thread decodes a track a packet at a time to 16-bit samples, rounded as the game's Vorbis reader rounds them, and hands it over in quarter-second chunks, at most about a second ahead of playback, so a track is never decoded whole and never delays sound effects. The mixer plays the samples as stereo frames at its own rate, as the game's does: a track at another rate plays at 44,100 Hz and a mono track's samples pair into frames. A chunk not yet decoded leaves the callback silent; a track that stops decoding is named and ends. Music plays at master times `dev_master_volume` times the music volume times the track's volume, through the music compressor and then the master compressor, and continues across seeks and pauses; it stops when the view loses the audio.

## Ownership, continuity and silence

One view owns the process audio at a time; frames of other views are dropped before they are sent. The desktop gives it to the map last pressed, dragged or scrolled, else the first visible map in layout order. A change of owner silences the previous one. A map that stops drawing (hidden, scrolled out, or another map's turn) releases the audio, which stops its sounds and music. Mute and volume changes are sent at once, not with the next frame.

The device callback only takes whole rendered frames from a lock-free queue and never waits; an underrun is silence for whole frames, so channels never shift. A sample that fails to load, or whose loader stopped, is refused by name and counted; later starts of that sound are refused too. A decoder panic refuses its file and leaves the loader running. The stream's error callback only records the error's kind in an atomic. An error the stream reports as leaving it playing (a reroute to the new default device on backends that follow it, an underrun, a refused real-time priority) changes nothing. Any other error, or a device that stops taking samples, drops the device and reports it in the status; mixing goes on by the clock, and the default device, or one that failed to open, is opened again after three seconds. On Windows the output backend reports a change of default device as an invalidated or unavailable stream, so that change takes this path and the new default device opens. The status lock is never held while a device opens.

Presentation discontinuities follow the viewer's [continuity rules](../viewer/README.md#playback-threading-and-frame-budgets): a seek, step back, branch change, gap or recreated units silence every sound effect and do not count present events as fired, so a seek or a long skip never bursts. Forward playback and its skips keep sounding loops. Pause keeps the entity clocks, so idle loops continue as the game's do. Dumps and exports never create the audio; a window capture creates it only as a silent trace when `BATTLE_PLAN_VIEWER_AUDIO_LOG` is set ([Logging](#logging)), never opening a device.

Frames send messages only. The control thread renders about 60 ms ahead in the mixer's 1,024-frame callbacks; the loader threads decode samples on first need, and a voice waits for its samples where the game's loader reads them at once. Decoded sound samples stay while used and for 60 seconds of mixed audio after their last use; a music track holds only its chunks waiting to play.

## Limits

- Alerts, interface clicks and voice-overs are absent because the viewer has no game interface for them to belong to: they sound the game's own windows and notifications, not the map.
- The out-of-range distance takes no margin: the game adds one only in its developer audio debug mode.
- Music decodes with Symphonia rather than the game's Vorbis library, so decoded samples can differ from the game's in their last bits.
- A sound's `pan`, which no installed content uses, is refused by name and counted, as are WAVE files of more than two channels and devices of three, five or seven channels.
- The game keeps sounding while unfocused; no unfocused-mute setting was found.

## Logging

Set `BATTLE_PLAN_VIEWER_AUDIO_LOG` to a file path to log every started voice and song (mixed-audio seconds, hour, effect, sound, position and falloff gain) and, every five seconds, a `#` line with the hour, emitters, instances, sounding voices, device and refusals. With it, a `--window-dump` capture traces what it would play without opening a device.

## Tests

`cargo test -p viewer-audio` runs synthetic fixtures: layered sound assets and generated WAVE files, rendered offline through the engine (including polyphony, the 32-voice mix, the voice slots, fades, rates, compressors, samples that fail to load and restarting emitters), the SDL conversions of samples and of the device stream, the music player's draws and streamed tracks, the battle ambience's emitters and their identity, and the system's control thread without a device: ownership, release, between-frame volumes, unreadable samples and stopped loaders, decoder panics, the whole-frame device queue, stream errors and a lost device.
