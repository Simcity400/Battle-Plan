# Collector

`collector` is the DLL the recorder injects into `hoi4.exe`. It captures scheduled native data at the hourly sampling boundary, then hands owned payloads to off-thread decoding, diffing and replay output.

Native layouts, offsets and guarded readers belong in [`engine`](../engine/README.md). Replay schemas, cadence and encoding belong in [`replay`](../replay/README.md).

## What to collect

Collect the least data the viewer and dashboard need to display, so recording costs the game as little as possible. Record the value the game already stores for what is displayed. Capture inputs instead only when the game does not store the result (it computes it on demand), when the result depends on the viewing country or camera, when it is text the game has already localised (display text follows the viewer's language, not the recording client's), or when it is much larger than its inputs. Every new table, field or cadence increase must serve something visible and be measured against its capture cost.

## How an hour is captured

[hook.rs](src/hook.rs) detours `CGameState::HourlyUpdate` at its entry. The game mutates state immediately after that entry: **there is no free, stable interval after the trampoline**. `sampling.rs` resets the frame, takes the country snapshot, dispatches and joins acquisition jobs, then returns into the game's hourly update. Required native reads must finish before that return, including reads performed by workers.

This boundary holds the simulation thread, not every native writer. Graphics caches and the token dictionary retain their targeted consistency checks. A detected overlapping change makes the affected data unavailable; capture never waits for those writers or retries their reads.

There are two capture paths. **Raw-copy jobs** stage spans in a `RawLog`; the decode thread replays the typed walk against owned memory. The pack's `raw_mode` tells `capture_tables` not to repeat that walk against live memory. **Typed-walk jobs** fill pack buffers directly, borrowing them through `take_*_request` and returning them through `restore_*_request`. Packs own their rows and arenas; conversion happens off the game thread.

Owned copies must be sufficient for deferred work: decoding must never follow live game pointers. Guard every native read, and preserve source order, identity checks, meaningful pointer occurrences, bounds and the first independent failure. Address equality alone does not identify a table or read occurrence; shared acquisition and raw-log lookups must retain each consumer's scope, including empty and overflow paths.

A failed or short read is not an empty table, a zero value or a complete row. Never call engine functions to obtain values, wait for memory to change or retry to hide a failure.

## Scheduling, buffers and failures

Keep typed walks in pool jobs. Document necessary game-thread exceptions beside the code; for example, the state-flags walk depends on the States rows. Dispatch according to dependencies, not merely available workers:

| Dependency | Dispatch rule |
|---|---|
| Live memory only | Eligible for the first dispatch burst; use the appropriate ordinary job. |
| Shared division inputs | Wait until the input packet is available to its consumers. |
| Raw-copy verdicts used by `GatedPacks` | Join the required raw jobs first, then dispatch `GatedPacks`. |
| Map-sample visitors, such as wing sources and order roots | Dispatch `LatePacks` after the map sample. |

**The Gated ordering prevents a specific ownership bug.** Multi-pack jobs swap the live packs with spare sets. Dispatching before a required raw verdict is installed makes the flag land on the spare set, not the pack already loaned to the worker; that pack then walks without the verdict.

`Role::priority()` orders queued jobs by their dependencies, lower first and FIFO among equals. The independent character role jobs start early enough to overlap later dispatch, although their results are joined at the typed-fallback boundary. Short, early-joined jobs must not wait behind large, late-joined jobs; the exception is the longest hourly jobs (World, Governance, Research), which claim first because the block cannot end before they do. With few workers, early-joined short jobs can then wait for one of them, so check the callback join waits on a constrained machine when changing this order. A join waits only for its own job: it must not execute unrelated queued work, and jobs must not submit other jobs. The pool has one worker per logical CPU in the game's process affinity, less one for the callback. A join spins on its own running ticket, for at most the recorder's per-hour budget, while another worker is idle, because a parked game thread can wait for a free CPU long after its job finishes; with every worker busy it parks at once. The decode and spool threads run below normal priority except while the capture buffers are backlogged, when the game thread may be waiting for them; `background_priority` in `status.json` counts the changes. The collector never changes a game thread's priority or affinity.

Tables with one or a fixed few rows per country slot are sized from the live country registry, never from a vanilla count: once before the hook is installed and again whenever the spool thread sees the registry grow (a save loaded after attaching at the main menu fills it before its first hourly tick). [country_scale.rs](src/country_scale.rs) owns the per-table factors; the same observation presizes the country authentication cache, which the hook swaps in without allocating, and the raw-copy job censuses. Nothing caps the country count below the engine's own u16 slot ceiling.

Reuse spare sets and return loaned buffers through the matching restore path. Their boxes hold allocation-free vacant packs while parked; the real preallocated packs move into the worker and back. Do not fill spare placeholders with default-capacity buffers. Keep allocation and capacity growth off the game thread.

Keep these outcomes distinct:

| Outcome | Required behaviour |
|---|---|
| Submission rejected, such as a full pool | Run inline exactly once at that job's join, except worker-only Governance, Orders, Ordinary and Presentation: publish `Unavailable` without live reads. |
| Accepted job panics or is stopped | Terminal for that hour: publish `Unavailable` with the failure reason, do not retry live reads, and do not convert partial rows. |
| Raw staging overflows | Grow staging capacity off-thread. Before a prepared source observation, the existing typed fallback is allowed; afterward, fail the capture without repeating live reads. This is not an accepted-job panic or stop. |
| Bounded walk truncates | Publish `Truncated { len, unread_rows }`, preserve growth demand and grow buffers off-thread. |

A Rust panic in callback coordination stops acquisition for that attachment. The incomplete buffer remains owned until unload; subsequent hours become explicit losses while the loader restores the hook, drains completed captures and reports the terminal failure.

The longest hourly jobs leave first: ShipModels is submitted first (its claim priority is last, but workers are idle when it arrives), then World, Research and, off the Focus slot, Governance at the highest claim priority. Governance otherwise waits for the World join, which decides whether World delegated that hour's Focus walk; off that slot nothing is delegated. Governance, Orders, Ordinary and Presentation each have a reserved pool slot and batch ticket. Their variant, building, order, combat and membership payloads can grow from already-read counts during acquisition, so the callback must never execute them, including fallback and unwind paths. Growth keeps the original observation; it does not restart the walk. Restore Gated before Governance returns its building buffers to construction; consume the building and variant timings once during final table assembly.

## Cadence and freshness

[cadence.rs](../replay/src/cadence.rs) is the authority. Map data is hourly, with established four-hourly exceptions for slow-changing inputs such as relations and province names. Buildings are hourly: bombing changes a building's mesh and fire within the hour. Character identities and unit leaders are hourly; persistent character roles and traits use their shared daily slot. Other panel data uses daily slots too. Keep shared-source families together and preserve the approved cadence when comparing capture implementations.

Static means the **whole table** cannot change after a save loads. Static tables are captured on full hours: branch start, keyframe, load or gap. Character portraits remain hourly because new generals can appear even when an existing character's portrait tokens do not change.

A sliced table (`Cadence::Sliced`) is refreshed one owner slice per hour, so no hour reads the whole table. Owners are native country IDs or native state IDs, one kind per family. A family is registered in [slicing.rs](src/slicing.rs) (`FAMILIES`, with its owner kind and tables) and has its own `SliceSelection` per frame and its own `SliceFingerprints` store for the attachment, so a country and a state with the same ID never share a bit. Each hour the family reads the owners of that hour's slice (`cadence::slice_active`), every owner on a full hour, and every owner whose change fingerprint differs from its last observation, including an owner seen for the first time (a new or re-aliased ID) and every owner after the store is forgotten. The fingerprint is family-specific and only adds reads. A read that is selected but not published (a failed or truncated table, a failed job, or a frame dropped before decode) is invalidated so its owners are read again at the next hour; a failed job forgets the store. Owners above the fingerprint range (the u16 slot range) are read in their slice only and are counted in `status.json` `slices.<family>`. Rows carry the `sample_hour` of their read; register the row type in [sliced.rs](../replay/src/sliced.rs) (`SlicedRow`, `merge_slice`). The spool merges each read into the previous complete table on the branch (only the read owners' rows are replaced, so the encoder emits changed rows only). The scan also records the live owners; after a complete census the merge drops the rows of owners it no longer sees, so a vanished or re-aliased owner's rows leave at the next hour rather than at its next slice. Without earlier rows it publishes the table unavailable and requests a full refresh. A truncated read replaces only the owners it returned rows for; the others keep their earlier rows in that truncated view, and because the view is not the base for the next merge, an owner's sample hour can move back by one hour in the following frame until its re-read completes.

Off-slot hours record `SKIPPED_REASON`; replay carries the previous data forward. Truncated staggered tables and failed static tables set `Shared.force_next`, forcing a full capture next hour with buffers grown off-thread. The lexer's maximum token ID is checked hourly; a change triggers another token-dictionary capture.

Record `DivisionEtaInputs` and let replay derive ETA. **Never source ETA operands from four-hourly or daily tables.**

## Reducing capture cost without changing data

**Reduce guarded calls, not just bytes.** `engine::Memory::copy` is an in-process, SEH-guarded copy. `copy_records` and `copy_scattered_prefetched<N>` gather multiple spans inside one guarded frame, avoiding repeated guard overhead. Divide per-table calls by row count to find repeated per-object work. Use a `const RecordLayout` for fixed-shape fields (`DIVISION_SEED_FIELDS` and `UNIT_ORDER_LAYOUT` are examples), scatter reads for independent fields, and `read_slice` for contiguous arrays. Batch predictable pointer enumeration; retain individual reads for unpredictable pointer chases. Preserve fault scope and ordering when batching.

**Reuse inputs at their proved lifetime.** Existing patterns include supply outer-map reuse (`InnerMemo`), ship doctrine and modifier metadata (`OrganisationMemo`, `DefinitionMemo`), province authentication (`ProvinceMemo`) and geometry (`StaticGeometry`). Key reuse by what invalidates it, such as module base, game state, play session or census header—not just a reused address. Reloadable defines, weather structures and mutable definitions are not automatically session-static. Shared inputs must preserve each consumer's identity and failure behaviour.

The character reader prepares its validated read geometry on recycle in [raw_plan.rs](src/raw_plan.rs). Each execution still acquires fresh character, role and leader values, including both name strings. Changed controls resume the ordinary producer from the exact acquired prefix; a failed native read is never retried. Prepared metadata and memory hints remove repeated discovery work without assuming that names or role definitions are immutable. The trait capture also retains the vector-source bindings selected by those checked controls. It freshly acquires every trait vector, definition and experience value on its scheduled refresh; only a complete acquisition at matching capacities can seed those bindings. Partial or exhausted binding storage keeps the ordinary producer, and allocation stays on recycle. Empty fields create no source observation and are omitted from prepared programs; nonempty observations retain their order, table scope and exact failure continuation.

Independent jobs acquire the character roster and unit leaders, country-leader roles, advisor roles, and traits. Each has its own reusable storage and prepared program. The decode worker merges only the tables owned by each part; a failed part cannot erase another part or retry a failed native read. The extra roster reads are included in accounting. Only the roster is joined before portraits; role and trait verdicts wait until the typed-fallback boundary, letting unrelated jobs start sooner. Worker durations overlap: measure the callback wall directly, rather than summing table timings.

The ordinary advisor job executes historical and generated rosters sequentially with separate prepared programs. Both current roster headers are authenticated before either body; growth or relocation of one vector invalidates only that section's geometry. Every pointer, character identity, advisor map, role and string is acquired fresh, including inactive roles. Owned decoding retains section scope, original occurrence order and the first failure. Exhaustion after an earlier prepared source observation cannot restart the table against live memory. Section diagnostics distinguish complete prepared executions from rediscovery; neither section is an extra scheduled job.

The default `advisor_changes` feature uses an owned per-character definition cache in [characters/advisors.rs](src/extended/characters/advisors.rs). Each scheduled acquisition freshly checks the complete roster, identities, countries, map headers and sentinels. Only unchanged entries with uninterrupted observed mutation revisions reuse their role rows and literal bytes; current country values are applied independently. Historical and generated rosters have independent prepared control plans. An unchanged section certifies its owned result in one guarded execution; a changed or failed control supplies its already-observed prefix to rediscovery without retrying memory. Plans are compiled on recycle after a complete, fully watched capture, including the first one. Role payload reads are omitted from control discovery and retain the native batched source path; their reuse is certified by mutation revisions. Country values are still read and fault-checked on every pass, but are applied to owned rows as fresh data instead of triggering equality-control rebuilds. Each country binding preserves its exact roster occurrence. Generated-roster growth cannot invalidate the historical plan. Pointer-selected prefetch hints overlap independent memory latency and remain behind their selecting control comparisons; they acquire no data. All characters and roles remain included. Separate source and destination arenas let one role grow without invalidating later characters' cached bytes. Capacity growth and replay conversion stay off the callback. The payload pool retains these typed buffers even though they have no raw-log spans.

[advisor_changes.rs](src/advisor_changes.rs) observes the pinned construction, destruction, role-field and map mutation boundaries documented in [advisor_mutations.rs](../engine/src/advisor_mutations.rs). Shared map helpers only notify exact registered map addresses. Notifications perform bounded atomic bookkeeping and, for role receivers, one guarded owner lookup; they do not capture or serialize roles. A quiet whole-domain revision avoids redundant per-character watch lookups. Loads, gaps, recovery and watch failures revoke reuse; detachment drains these callbacks before freeing their code. `advisor_verify` freshly reads every proposed hit and checks exact rows, byte content and order. Mismatches disable reuse for the attachment. Diagnostic status records verification, changed/reused work and notification overhead separately from capture wall time. Exercised-path verification does not substitute for native writer coverage. The optional `advisor_profile` feature retains a bounded set of per-section wall ticks, thread cycles and prepared-read stop details, including initial attachment; it adds no timing calls to the default path.

Each character job reserves storage for the tables it owns; only the trait owner allocates trait definitions and occurrence buffers. Initial raw and preparation capacities follow the job's shape. Larger captures retain the existing bounded fallback and off-thread growth paths, rather than imposing a smaller data limit.

A complete prepared acquisition loans its immutable span geometry to owned decoding by swapping buffers. Recycle returns the geometry before clearing or retiring the plan; no descriptor list is copied on the callback. Source values remain fresh. Character acquisition diagnostics distinguish prepared backend wall time from the remaining capture work; these overlapping job intervals are not an additive callback breakdown.

Capture-buffer reuse depends on the entire downstream pipeline, not just decode queue depth. After conversion has produced owned replay input and diagnostic snapshots, the spool worker grows and returns the capture buffer before encoding and writing. A truncated input retains its buffer until a successful write publishes the required full-refresh request. Prepared input must be written before preparing another frame or flushing because comparison tokens refer to the current encoder baseline. The spool worker reports buffer service through recycling and idle-flush wall time separately. Periodic disk synchronization runs on a bounded durability worker; initial segments, forced boundaries and detach still wait for completed synchronization. File-write delays can still hold up conversion of queued frames and exhaust the finite capture pool, so buffer-wait counters and callback tails remain required performance checks.

The roster job also owns a bounded [portrait census](../engine/src/ccharacter_manager.rs): authenticated pointer/identity occurrences with successful country reads, in roster order. The callback joins only this producer before dispatching portrait shares, allowing portrait reads to overlap the remaining character jobs, and loans only a complete current census. A successful prepared character pass certifies the retained occurrence sequence through fresh pointer and identity checks. A partial diagnostic pass preserves that association only when every occurrence is identical; a changed census retires the old plan. Without an admitted census, portraits take their independent primary path. Every loan returns before the character job can be recycled.

The `portrait_changes` feature observes native portrait writers and lifetimes in [portrait_changes.rs](src/portrait_changes.rs), with pinned writer evidence in [ccharacter_portraits.rs](../engine/src/ccharacter_portraits.rs). Every hourly occurrence still has fresh ownership, identity, country readability and array-header validation. Only descriptor entries with the same owner, arena position and uninterrupted quiet mutation revision can reuse owned bytes. The bounded watch registry keeps exact addresses; collisions never invalidate unrelated entries, and watch exhaustion falls back to fresh descriptor reads. Native callbacks execute the original function and only update atomic metadata.

[snapshot_plan.rs](src/snapshot_plan.rs) prepares portrait dependency checks only from a fully reused warm walk, so a cold descriptor read cannot accidentally become permanent hourly work. A quiet, unchanged pass retains the owned sorted rows. Shared-census passes first match every current pointer/identity occurrence, then acquire fresh headers with bounded lookahead hints. Partial or failed walks invalidate their cache. Loads, gaps and recovery invalidate the observer epoch; a continuous encoding keyframe retains certified native inputs while still emitting a complete replay snapshot.

`portrait_verify` retains the ordinary descriptor reads and compares every proposed hit with current data. A mismatch permanently disables reuse for that attachment. Its counters live in `status.json`, outside replay data. This checks exercised paths, not unexercised writer coverage. The observer is disabled before its detours are removed, and callback return paths drain before their trampolines or DLL can be freed. A future rolling refresh needs explicit observation provenance; it cannot use this mutation-certified current-value contract to label older unchecked rows as fresh.

**Split only measured bottlenecks.** Workers contend for cores and memory; more parallelism is not automatically faster. For country-grouped walks, interleave outer units by every n-th slot rather than taking contiguous ranges, because large countries occupy neighbouring slots. Each share repeats header and first-fault work. Keep separate rows and arenas per `Part`, put once-per-family work in part 0, and restore single-walk row order at conversion. Merge outcomes so any `Unavailable` wins; otherwise sum lengths and unread counts. Preserve duplicate occurrences and capacity demand.

## Changing a capture path

**Add a table.** Add its replay row and ID in [rows.rs](../replay/src/rows.rs) or [extended_rows.rs](../replay/src/extended_rows.rs), its cadence entry, and an `extended/<pack>.rs` pack with `N`, `TABLES`, `READ_GROUPS`, `Capacities` and `Captured`'s `capture`, `outcomes` and `tables`. Register it in `packs!` in [extended/mod.rs](src/extended/mod.rs), then wire acquisition and conversion through the appropriate job. Change the [format](../replay/src/format.rs) version when the wire or recorded observation semantics change. Only the current format is supported; update its writers and consumers together.

**Move a pack into a job.** Wire its `Captured` through the matching multi-pack struct, `spare()`, `capture`, `swap_*` and take/restore paths; remove the former inline walk without breaking the failure distinctions above. A dedicated job also needs `Job`, `Report`, `Role`, `read_anchor`, `priority`, `ROLE_COUNT`, `ROLES`, `Job::role`, and dependency-correct dispatch/join points in `sampling.rs`.

**Change native readers in `engine`.** Read the [reverse-engineering guide](../../tools/re/README.md) first. Keep one source file per native struct, with evidence beside constants: native writer, serializer or function, width, signedness and confirmation. Use `C:\Tools\hoi4_mcp\hoi4re.cmd` and save established names, fields and notes. If decompilation drops arguments or arithmetic, inspect indexed instructions; distinguish virtual addresses from image-relative RVAs. Old readers, including `archive/pre-rewrite-2026-09-09`, are research leads, not proof. Stored fields, calculated getters and renderer caches are different sources; similar save values are not interchangeable evidence.

## Testing and measurement

Use existing synthetic-memory fixtures to check aliases, invalid pointers, short reads, exact first failures, capacity and growth demand, and owned-versus-direct equivalence. Share splits repeat first-fault reads and cache keys add reads; update counted expectations deliberately. Run `cargo test -p collector` and `cargo test -p engine` while iterating, then the full fmt, Clippy, build and workspace-test checks in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md). New capture domains need collector/replay integration and an oracle comparison or an explicit limitation. See the [recorder guide](../recorder/README.md#inspect-and-compare) for hourly-sample versus later-save limits.

Use [tools/bench.ps1](../../tools/bench.ps1), frozen builds and a same-run no-recorder baseline. Do not compile during timed windows or trust throughput percentages from a locked desktop or collapsed baseline. Acquire `%TEMP%\claude\hoi4_game.lock\owner` before controlling the game; release it by renaming the lock directory to `hoi4_game.lock.released-<stamp>-<why>`. Leave the game running and paused.

The blocking acquisition time is the primary diagnostic. In the documented instrumentation, `status.json` exposes `acquisition_work.mean_ns` (`collect_wait`), `block_phases.segments_mean_ns` and `join_wait_by_role_mean_ns`; `SEGMENTS_SCOPE` names the segments. Inspect p50, p95 and maximum as well as means. Earlier benchmark evidence found extra smoothing frames during long ticks, with a threshold around a 16 ms block; remeasure that threshold rather than treating it as universal.

The optional `callback_profile` feature retains a bounded set of slow hourly callbacks in `status.json`, with the game hour, wall time, game-thread cycles, disjoint acquisition phases, fixed segments and job-join times. It records no extra native game reads, takes no lock and allocates no storage on the callback. Cycles are not milliseconds; compare them with wall time to investigate waiting or descheduling. A join that spins on its running job (see Scheduling) counts as game-thread cycles, not as waiting; the pool's `spins` counter in `status.json` shows how often that happened. The default build has no profiling calls. Per-hour evidence is needed to explain a particular spike; cumulative means cannot reconstruct it.

Skipped hours contribute zero to per-table means. For a table captured once every 24 hours, its per-walk cost is 24 times the all-hour mean; extra full captures change that frequency. Compare job durations per completed call, not just averaged join waits. The documented `workers.roles[].ticks` uses 10 MHz ticks; use the checked-out branch's actual units and counter definitions.

Attribute native reads with `read_accounting::table(TableId)` and keep `table_ns` timings truthful. Decoding owned memory is not another native read. Performance counters belong in `status.json`, not replay schemas or the spool; do not add runtime verification windows or second reads to those outputs.

Record measured block and throughput changes in the commit or PR, separately from fixture read-count savings. Keep simple, demonstrated reductions in work even when throughput variance hides their individual effect; say what was proved. Run the relevant fresh-save oracle and existing smoke workflow from the recorder guide. Faster incomplete capture is not an improvement. Keep benchmark histories, current hotspot rankings and migration progress out of this guide.

Research inputs, applied-bonus attribution and modifier breakdowns belong to the governance family but run in their own Research job, which walks its own country census. They are the `research` sliced family, owned by countries. The fingerprint (`engine::research_inputs::fingerprint_country`) covers every research slot, each slot's technology, applied days and speed, and each assigned technology's level, classifier and applied bonuses, but not daily progress, banked days or modifier sources. So a new, cancelled or completed assignment, a slot added or removed, and a new country are read within the hour, while a change that only moves a research modifier (an idea, law, advisor, decision, scientist, licence or sharing change) waits for the country's slice. hoi4re records the writers (research audit 2026-09-25): research is not daily-only. That keeps the family's largest hourly walk off the Governance critical path; the rows return into the family before its tables are published, and a failed Research job cannot erase the rest of the family. Rows are preallocated and converted outside capture; the bonus roster is read once per country only when applied IDs need resolution, and the modifier table fails independently of inputs and bonuses. Synthetic memory tests cover read calls, failure isolation and truncation.
