# Equipment stockpile

For each equipment type, whether one country has a surplus or a shortage, as the game's Logistics screen shows it, and what it holds and makes per week (mock-up economy-1). The game computes the surplus only for player-controlled countries; for an AI-controlled one the widget shows exact stock and production with the note "The game doesn't compute surplus or shortage for AI-controlled countries."

- **Bars (default view):** one row per group, sliding to a new order (the kit's moving rows); bars glide over the app's motion time within one unbroken stretch of recording and jump across a gap; numbers are exact at once.
  - **Player-controlled** (the hours the game computed the balance): the signed-off diverging bars. Each row's surplus (right) or shortage (left, red) as a share of what is needed, all rows on one scale, so small and large types compare fairly; the exact surplus or shortage at the bar's end ("+1,549", "−373"). A "shortage | surplus" header sits above the zero line. A row whose need cannot be derived (see [Data](#data)), or that needs nothing, has no share, so it draws no bar and shows only its exact amount, muted.
  - **AI-controlled, before the balance was recorded, or by variant:** a bar from zero to what is in stock, with the exact amount. A recorded negative stock (the game keeps some) is drawn left of zero in red. The caption says why no surplus shows.
  - **Order (setting):** game order, biggest shortage first (most negative surplus first; rows without one keep the game's order after), or most in stock first (by the stock the row shows: the logistics record's where the surplus is shown, else the stockpile's).
  - **Bar length (setting):** one scale over the whole campaign (the largest share at any read), so bars never rescale during playback, or fit the moment shown.
  - The caption says when the values shown were read.
- **Over time (setting):** labels name a variant as recorded at the hour: the tooltip at the hovered hour, the chips and legend at the hour shown, each end label at its line's last hour. The percentage axis is labelled by the kit's `formatAxisPercent`, so small ranges keep their ticks apart. For a country with a recorded balance, each chosen group's surplus as a percentage of need, broken where the country was AI-controlled, where the record was not read or where its need cannot be derived; the value axis holds zero, which is emphasised, and follows the kit's log rule (log scale only where every share shown is positive), and names sit at the line ends (a setting; without them a legend lists each line's value at the playhead). Without any recorded balance (AI throughout, an older recording, or by variant) it draws what was in stock.
  - Each grouping keeps its own chosen lines (types, variants and arms are saved apart), so switching grouping never disables the other's chips. A variant is saved with its campaign (the kit's `campaignToken`, from the reply's `campaignKey`) as well as its handle and equipment token. A handle is the game's equipment id, so it names one design only within its campaign, and even there a reload of an earlier save restores the game's variant index, so a later branch can give the handle to another design. The choice therefore also stores the design recorded by the hour shown (the product's fingerprint of its name, modules and recorded upgrade levels, read with the variant table). It selects the variant only where that handle has had the saved design in this branch (a rename changes the fingerprint, so the branch's whole history is matched); another design under the handle is shown as an unavailable "saved design" chip ("This design isn't in this branch") and never drawn in its place. A variant whose design was never recorded cannot be chosen.
  - With included types chosen, a row's completeness depends only on what they admit: an unresolved type is an archetype of its own, keyed `:` and its recorded token (a key settings can store, so it can be included and chosen; the arm of unresolved types is keyed `unresolved`), so its key decides whether it is included, and an excluded one never makes a row unknown; a variant of unknown type could belong to any included type, so it still does.
  - Chips above the chart choose the groups, up to eight (the kit's `chosenShown` and `toggleChosen`). Only what can be drawn is chosen: a group with no line (a type whose need cannot be derived, such as supply trucks, in the share chart) keeps its chip, unavailable with why, and the settings offer only drawable groups for the country and grouping; where nothing can be drawn at all, the chart says why instead of drawing an empty plot. Like Included types, each choice is stored as `setToken(key)` (read through `storedChoices`), so a type whose key a setting cannot store, such as a mod's `mod/rifles`, can be chosen too. Until one is chosen, the main land and air types show (the three land and two air types with the most in stock or needed at any read; the five variants with the most; every arm), among those that have something to draw. A type with no share to draw says so on its chip.
  - The keyboard steps through the reads as in every time chart; the tooltip gives each line's surplus or shortage with its share, what is needed and produced per week (or its stock).
- **Grouping (setting):** by equipment type (default; the game's archetypes from content, so a mod's types appear by themselves, including shortages of types the country holds none of), by exact variant, or by land, air and naval. **Included types (setting)** limit the rows and lines to chosen types; empty shows every type recorded.
  - **By variant**, rows show stock and production only: the game counts surplus and need by equipment type, not by variant.
  - **By arm**, a row sums every type of the arm the record lists (zeros included, in any order), and its need only when every type's need is derived (land includes trucks and trains, whose need cannot be, so it has no share); the game's own category rows also add incoming lend-lease, which is not recorded, as its tooltip says.
- **Hover** (or keyboard focus on a row), for a player-controlled country: in stock (as the same daily record counted it), needed, surplus or shortage, share of need, produced per week, then the variants in stock (as many as fit the window, at most eight, the rest summed; the kit's `fitTooltip`, whose spoken text lists every variant). For an AI-controlled country: in stock, produced per week and the note. A screen reader gets each row's surplus and share (or stock) in its label and the full tooltip as its description.
- **Right-click** (or the context-menu key on a row) jumps to that moment: the hour under the pointer, or the read the bars show.
- **Settings:** view, grouping, included types, the lines over time and their end labels, order, bar length, the time chart's playback mode, window and axes.

## Data

**Product `equipment-stockpile`** (`crates/dashboard/src/products/equipment_stockpile.rs`), for one country over the branch:

- each archetype's recorded `LogisticsEquipment` balance (the Logistics screen's "Stored" surplus or deficit, `CLogisticsStatus` series 16) and the stock the same daily update counted (series 15), fixed5, read in daily slot 0 just after the game's daily update; the hours the balance is known, and the hours the country was AI-controlled (a complete table without its rows: the game writes the balance only when `IsCountryAIControlled` is false);
- runs of each variant's recorded `Stockpiles` amount, and of its military production lines' output per week, the sum of each line's units per week by the game's weekly formula (`speed × 700000 ÷ cost`, truncated; the game's own formatter shows that figure for rates between one unit a day and one a week, and the same rate per day, month or year otherwise; `products/equipment.rs`, shared with every equipment product). Naval, refit and railway-gun lines are not military lines and what they deliver to the stockpile is not established, so a variant one of them makes has an unknown weekly total at that read, never 0. A line without a positive cost has no rate (the game shows only a placeholder for it), so its variant's weekly total is unknown at that read, never zero or a partial sum;
- each variant's name as the game writes it (`GetDisplayName`), and each type's archetype, name, arm and whether its need can be derived, from content (`installed_content::equipment`, the one equipment reader the map's land models, aircraft, raids and the save oracle also read).

**What is needed** is derived from the record, never approximated. The game stores the balance as `Avail − clamp(Demand + PoolDifference)`, where `Demand` is what training lines, reinforcement requests, garrisons, air wings, operations, trucks and trains need, and `PoolDifference` is net outgoing lend-lease. `Avail` is the recorded stock clamped at zero, except for convoy types (the live pool), `supply_truck` types (plus the supply system's trucks) and train types (less its trains). So for every other type, `needed = max(0, stock) − balance` exactly; for those three kinds the terms are not recorded and the need, and so a share, is not shown (the tooltip says why), and a sum at the game's saturation bound (±`0x53e2d620fb40`) is not a need. The surplus or shortage itself is the game's own number for every type.

A recorded zero is a value (a type recorded at zero shows 0, not "Not recorded"), a value with no run inside a known span had no row, and outside every span it is unknown. The balance is always shown with the stock of its own record; the stockpile and lines come from their own daily read. Each is carried until its next read, a value read earlier than the hour shown says when it was read, and a gap, a missing table or a change of country identity ends them.

A variant is named as the read in force at the hour recorded it (the kit's `entryAt`); before its first recorded name, and from a gap, a due variant read that did not land, or an hour whose carried variant table is not complete, until the next read (the product's `VariantCatalogue`), it is named by its equipment token, else its handle. A type or arm row adds up variants, so it is unknown (never zero or partial) wherever it cannot tell them all: when it holds a type content does not resolve, or when any variant of unknown type (its definition unread or unresolved) holds a nonzero or unknown value then, since it could belong to any row. Likewise a resolved arm's surplus, stock, need and share are not known ("Not known", with why) while a type content does not resolve holds a surplus or stock, since it could belong to that arm; a type's own row, and the arm of unresolved types, stay exact. Variant rows stay exact. A share of need is kept exact, so a tiny surplus is never drawn as none ("+0.00005%").

**Arms** come from the engine's own classifiers of each type's category flags: the plane family is air, the ship family (convoys included) naval, the rest land.

The surplus and shortage bars and chart are tested with synthetic frames and node tests, and were checked in the browser against a live recording with a human and AI countries, value by value against its save.

**Built from the kit:** tooltips are fitted, rows slide and describe themselves, bars diverge from zero, chips choose, lines are styled, runs cut and dated, and end labels placed by the shared chart and dashboard kit (see [Reuse first](../README.md#reuse-first)); this folder keeps only what the stockpile's values mean and how its rows read.

## Mod compatibility

- Equipment types, their archetypes, database order, arms and need rules come from the recorded content stack (`installed_content::equipment`: vanilla, DLC, then mods in order, `duplicate_archetypes` included; references and recorded spellings resolve under the lexer's ASCII fold), so a mod's types appear and removed vanilla ones vanish. The default chart lines are chosen from recorded amounts, never by name.
- Arms are the engine's own plane and ship classifiers of a type's category flags (cited beside the code); their labels (Land, Air, Naval) are the widget's own words for that fixed engine grouping, not content.
- Names come from localisation through the stack (a variant's own name, then `<TAG>_<key>`, then `<key>`) and fall back visibly: a type to its key, a variant to its token, then "Variant <id>". Art is a visible placeholder.
- Equipment reads through the native database reader, including its variable, alias and redefinition rules. Native-reader diagnostics name the affected file and definition; unsupported loading refuses the database, so types and every total they could belong to stay unknown. A recorded token no layer declares is refused by name. An unknown category keyword adds nothing, as the engine reports and skips it.
- A resolved arm's need stays unknown while a type content does not resolve is listed, even at zero: its stock may hold unrecorded supply terms.

## Not yet

- **Equipment art** is a neutral placeholder for now. The game draws a type's row with the country's best variant for it and that variant's icon. The recording holds both inputs: the best-variant candidates are `AvailableEquipmentVariants` roster kind 2, and each variant's icon entry is in `VariantDefinitions`. The widget does not read them yet; it needs the shared equipment icon rule and the best-variant ranking.
- **Need for convoys, trucks and trains** is not derived: the supply system's truck and train counts and the live convoy pool are not recorded.
- **Comparing countries** is left out: the widget shows one country.
