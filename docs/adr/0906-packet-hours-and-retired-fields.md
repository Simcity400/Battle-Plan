# 0906. Keep proven packet hours and retired field framing

Hourly host rows prove the acquisition time of their nested packets. Frame carry applies to staggered tables, so a complete or truncated hourly host captures only the current hour's packets. An unread packet leaves an explicit refusal. Writers omit the repeated hour, allowing identical acquisitions to encode as unchanged and the decoder to establish freshness after a keyframe or seek. Through format 101, a packet carries its own read hour and the reader checks that stamp against the frame. Format-101 test writers encode the stamp and the older retired parts so the fixtures exercise the layouts their headers name.

An ended capture keeps its field position and framing when those are needed to read the rest of an older row. A whole opaque `Retired` value skips the old payload through the field's outer length. It would consume the enclosing payload if used for one part of a nested value. `RetiredNested<T>` instead decodes the previous part at its original position and discards it. `RetiredKept<T>` decodes and retains that part for a consumer with a historical fallback. Current nested tombstones emit zero bytes. A fixed-width tombstone retains the old width and delta semantics. Current writers emit its zero, false, or absent value. `RetiredU32List` emits an empty list. Older fixed-width and list values still participate in delta decoding, keeping subsequent fields readable.

The reserved transport-preview singleton discarded its inline packet at format 102. The provisional split into keyed tables and the nearest-base caches never appeared in a published format. Refuse those tables by name in every recording. Keep their identifiers and type framing reserved. The inline format-101 packet is skipped using its opaque length, so its internal transport and spotting operands have no playback consumers.

The table registry points to typed operations compiled by the table owners. The replay table crates each compile one partition to avoid instantiating every table's operations in the same crate. The dispatch is once per table and operation, with the row loop inside the typed function.
