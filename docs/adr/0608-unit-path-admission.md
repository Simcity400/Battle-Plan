# Match unit movement rules at the point where each route needs them

Ordinary destination admission, a route search, and a forced transfer answer different questions. A move into enemy-held territory can qualify for admission under disclosure, access or a shared enemy. A route search still applies its own restrictions on traversing that territory. Targeted reach queries must agree with a complete search. Reaching a province behind an unknown edge can settle a target only if that edge actually affects the route to it. The resumable search preserves this distinction when paused during either phase. The fixtures compare it against a plain flood fill and exercise all outcomes.

Invasion routes preserve the game's signed edge costs, non-admissible heuristic and neighbour order. Costs can double when the destination controller's relation test fails, including the zero country slot. A zero edge blocks, while negative nonzero costs remain eligible. Invasion port links do not require a naval base or a passable strategic region. Normal embarkation and landing use active port bases, while the forced counter route checks base presence. Exile can bypass movement access while province admission still needs the start's state owner. A region gate is evaluated on entry from a different region.

Rail counter movement requires a directed rail connection on the rail path. A forced sea transfer uses port links without needing railway nodes or the unit's orders group. A border conflict checks the actual orders group at the admission gate. A unit starting within that conflict bypasses only the province access test. Task forces retain their own access and unit-class restrictions and never traverse ordinary land edges. Visible fleets can block straits through a sea province. An absent through-province remains unknown, and a land through-province supplies no fleet. Exile bypasses the visible strait fleet gate without bypassing class restrictions.

The cost and admission readers distinguish raw positive ownership or controller operands from their resolved country links. A raw nonpositive operand can skip a relation read, even when an alternate country link exists. Required-province conflict reasons use the first matching relation along each overlord chain. A subject tie participates in disclosure and a cycle withholds the answer. A contested rule can close an army crossing. Combat separately governs when a unit can disengage and whether its commander bars retreat. A whole unit's worth of enemy strength blocks retreat, and a defender cannot retreat into its attackers.
