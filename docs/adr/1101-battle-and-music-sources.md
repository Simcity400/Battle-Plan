# 1101. Use recorded battle membership and country music chances

Land battle ambience uses the recorded battle list in native visit order. A battle draws its sound at the recorded centre above the terrain. Its audible range is its recorded radius plus the distant effect's falloff. The total includes all units in its member provinces' combats, and only division template slots contribute to sub-unit layers. Repeated province membership contributes each time. A battle without enough units does not play. Qualifying sub-unit layers use authored count ranges. Missing division composition produces a named refusal and unreadable battle defines or an undeclared distant effect skip the update. The native battle object keys the looping presentation across changes in list position.

Music draws from enabled stations using the viewing country's song chances at the presented hour. A song's recent position scales its chance, with a positive floor for a drawable song and a fixed-point weight. A song outside the history keeps its whole chance. The local draw counter is independent of the effect mixer, and a change of song list clears its index history. A condition the recording cannot answer is reported and the song keeps its authored base chance. When a track ends, the next draw uses the last presented chances and opens immediately so the next callback can start it. The handoff waits once for the chosen track, and mixing resumes without blocking repeatedly on a slow decoder.

The music loader converts Vorbis samples to 16 bits in bounded chunks and leaves their decoded rate and channels intact. The mixer pairs samples into stereo at its own rate, including a mono track. If a chunk is late, the rest of that callback stays silent and the track still owns the player. End of stream and decode failure end playback. An unreadable track is skipped and a failed stream is reported by name once. Dropping the consumer stops work on its track.
