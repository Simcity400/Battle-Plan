# Publish owned event facts in acquisition order

An acquired event is known immediately when its provenance has already been proved. Otherwise its proof and ticket remain pending until the original settles. A seal leaves the pending event and its successors unpublished, preserving acquisition order even when nested calls finish in another order. Uncertain completion retains the entry, proof, flight and ticket. A failed post-read cannot retry a consumed proof. After a world invalidation, a normally returned original settles without reading an older proof. The stored event retains its original cause. A dispatch's instance binds only to its own acquired native frame, since an older publication can retain entries long after its dispatch returned.

Native occurrence storage grows from observed demand. Acquiring the one reached occurrence avoids a native queue walk. The callback retains the occurrence even when token allocation fails so its token can be marked unrecorded. A refusal marker must carry its static reason; a failed marker spends no local refusal control. Captured strings and scopes are owned before the hourly handoff, and encoding interns tokens off-thread. Per-frame witness storage can grow in a burst and reports that demand again for sizing before the next hour.

Census completion records its recovery epoch only after it visited every live owner without an intervening loss. A crossed sample or preparation shortage retries at a later visit. A stable native error keeps its own reason. Owner refusals name the first reason, count once and leave unrelated owners certified. A global observation loss retires the affected observations and starts a new recovery epoch. Recovery for a previously certified scalar does not establish a new certificate. A crossed scalar read error retries without treating overlap as a content failure.

The sequence seed includes previously retained branches and truncated prefixes. Receipt cells include seals that carried accounting without events and can be accepted, discarded or still in flight. Only the first publication covering an active failure can retire it. A newer loss is not acknowledged by an older healthy seal or an earlier refusal. During detach after the decode and spool workers join, an unresolved owner keeps the store alive. An unsealed tail has no acknowledgement or invented hour. Old allocations returned by storage handoff are dropped off the lease.

The decision index and staging arena prepare together because the arena holds decision and activation witnesses. Crossed-sample counters distinguish delayed, decision and registry acquisition. War observations retain inputs before the original and destruction before reentrant relation replacement. Annex delivery copies actor and FROM before authored dispatch can change them. Native string descriptors and pointers are acquisition-local.

## Windows event feed bridge

The Windows x64 `event_war_entry` and `event_annex_entry` CALL bridges wrap void targets. The war target takes status and candidate in RCX and RDX. The annex target takes the database, list, scope and zero in RCX, RDX, R8 and R9. Their callers use no stack operands or returned flags. The bridge preserves the original's volatile integer and XMM outputs through post-observation. Linked validation covers the unwind metadata and native ABI, CET and CFG requirements.

`EVENT_ENTRY` reserves 32 bytes of home space, the 224-byte `EventFrame` and 8 bytes for alignment after the return address. The Rust borrow requires the entire frame to be initialized, including `generation` when observation is refused. Only the opaque `MaybeUninit<Ticket>` storage may be uninitialized. The assembly, Rust `Frame` and C++ `EventFrame` share this layout. The Rust observer alone inspects and settles the ticket.

The entry attempts one checked flight acquisition. Contention, a saturated count or a preexisting retention bit leads to retention. A normal exit decrements only the flight acquired by that invocation. Its contribution stays outstanding throughout the guarded call, and no other path resets it or decrements it. `hoi4_event_forward` keeps the frame in RBX, restores native operands and XMM registers before calling the target, and saves the target's actual outputs before any further observer callback. A native escape unwinds through that frame to the C++ finalizer.

`hoi4_event_native_guard` isolates faults in the two injected observer callbacks. A fault in the first callback retains ownership, and the original runs once even if observation refuses or faults. The original executes outside both observer filters and its exceptions propagate to the native caller. On an abnormal exit the finalizer retains flights, tickets and storage. Flight decrement belongs to the normal assembly exit.
