# Carry equipment and map observations independently within the wing census

The hourly wing census still visits every wing because membership and cumulative inputs need it. The equipment store can carry a pool read when the wing address, ID and pool certificate match the read registered under that certificate. A fresh read records rows in pool order. Pool observers cover the runtime writer set, including retirement and the aircraft-loss notification forwarded from the equipment-destruction family. If the loss observer is disabled or absent, its writes cannot certify a hold, and admitting it again starts a new epoch. Pool authentication failure, an incomplete census, a load, a gap, a recovery, an observer table replacement, poison or an active writer invalidates any affected hold. Reusing an address for another wing does not carry the previous identity's rows.

A split census has an independent store per owner partition. A wing crossing partitions needs a fresh read in the destination. Settlement indexes each part and records its accounting before the census concludes. The game thread concludes every part together without allocation. Only a complete census keeps its results. Carried rows keep ranges into the previous buffer while fresh reads use separate scratch. A changed pass or a withdrawal merges those ranges once in census order. Duplicate wing identities cannot prove a unique held read, and allocation failures merely withhold carry.

Owner ranges are sized from the complete preceding census, whose cost is the sum of wings and admitted strategic-air owners. Each later part grows its own map, source, naval and score projections from its admitted headers. Workers build their own identities and naval memberships. The callback joins parts in owner order and stops a failed projection at the same point as the serial walk. A map or source refusal can leave the game-score leaves running; later outputs of a refused projection cannot enter that result. The earliest outcome in slot order wins. Identities require a complete source census. The join charges grown capacity to the receiving job even on refusal, and a failed acquisition forgets all stores, including idle parts whose allocations are retained. The first worker holds the joined capacity for future callbacks.

Map and raid flags use field-specific observer keys distinct from the pool keys. The first pass can read between unchanged quiet tokens while the watch registry has no receiver table, but that result contributes no reusable certificate. The asynchronous census prepares storage from its admitted wing count. An earlier training wing can become the first selected member of a group, so later members of a training group also need the stop-byte observation. Duplicate admissions cannot carry a flag as one identity.

Transfer feedback can select any pool-backed wing, independently of mission eligibility, and checks its raid source first. A held raid reference requires a matching receiver certificate and proof that the pointed-to object remains live. A liveness observation made without a receiver certificate is bounded by two quiet tokens and cannot carry further. Transfer feedback can reuse the immediately preceding accepted raw reference and liveness without another registry or object read. A retired identity cannot become live again in the same epoch merely by polling the registry.

Incoming registrations and hourly transfer progress use the mission job's complete wing and base witnesses. The direct vector certifies nonmembership when available. If the membership observation failed, the result instead retains an explicit unknown. A certified failed observation stays unknown until its token changes. Transfer demand groups destinations and census indices in one bulk arena, resolving the pool base and any override through one registry pass without duplicating idle demand for a registered wing.
