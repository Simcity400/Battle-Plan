# 0401. Follow the deployment queue and upgrade graph by ownership

The deployment manager owner's backlink authenticates a country's queue. Its selected staff template and next headquarters order also exist when the queue is empty. The staff selection retains its full reference even if that reference no longer resolves. Queue order is native state because conveyors and lines can move within compacted vectors. Conveyor and line handles retain both native words. A line owns its deployment and points back to its conveyor. The deployment's line backlink must equal the line being read. The queued template belongs to the conveyor.

Conveyor, line, manpower and in-transit equipment rows share one traversal and one structural failure boundary. A broken object withholds the queue. Buffer shortages are counted separately per table from authenticated parent headers, even when a parent row is omitted. A line that does not fit publishes no orphan children. Literally named rows do not truncate or borrow a name. The output keeps zero and negative signed manpower and fixed-point amounts, the raw zero-entry flag, and optional halting reason presence independently from its value. It preserves raw role tokens, closed flags, name order and custom name flags rather than normalizing them to display values. Nonpositive gated country references are absent. Empty names and a zero role token may be stored values. Save rules about omission or defaults do not convert live values to absence.

Follow an upgrade graph through the independently authenticated army census and its requests object. An empty request list can unregister a group even as deliveries remain. The requests object's persistent subobject and owner/status backlinks prove membership independently of the active registry. Every group and its children keep source order. Request and delivery positions are local to their group because no stable native ids are established. The worker sizes each lane from the acquired headers, without retries. Deliveries own their produced equipment pools. Progress is available only for the status that supplies it. A status fault withholds progress without invalidating an independently authenticated pool, and inactive progress spans require no read. Both raw upgrade quantities and progress keep their signed fixed-point representation.
