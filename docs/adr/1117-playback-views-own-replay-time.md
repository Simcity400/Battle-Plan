# Publish playback rows from the map side’s mirrors

Script evaluators and whole-map builders need typed rows in key order. A separate reader on its own cadence could describe a different hour from the side applying a step. `Views` builds rows from the same mirrors the side has just advanced, and assigns the frame the branch and hour actually applied. The dashboard’s independent follow host uses the same store in `Apart` and can apply a batch of steps at its own head. `Stepped` keeps its own ordered consumer when a reader needs an exact event-context hour even if the map takes several steps in one frame.

Each maker declares column sets. The union becomes the mirror’s demand, while a maker receives only its own sets. A mirror must hold all declared columns and stand at the requested hour or in a gap. A table the recording never wrote answers with a stable named absence. A column added after an older recording’s schema is likewise absent. Temporary gaps clear the view until recorded history resumes. The row decoder uses kind-zero placeholders internally for omitted columns, and the view boundary refuses direct reads of those columns. Missing text tokens are withheld instead of reconstructed. The map’s last usable product stays visible while its maker waits for complete inputs.

Sets keep stable row allocations when their columns are untouched. Insertions, deletions and status changes affect every set. Other writes affect only sets with a written column. Touched cells are first noted by slot, remapped when slots are packed, and converted to ordered keys when a view is made. A patch can reuse an unheld spare, copy the current allocation, or start again. Measured per-row costs choose among those paths. A reset, unkeyed rows, changed status, missing text, a large patch or a failed patch can require a whole view. Previous allocations survive while frames hold them. A panicking make withholds the table until a later changed step permits a fresh build.

The side queues one job per changed set and prioritizes the next maker with the longest measured build, with expensive sets first inside that maker. A maker can start once its sets are ready, while later makers’ views and the side’s independent layer work continue. Workers start lazily and park between posts. The side may take work itself and gets exclusive mirrors back only after every job releases them. The post provides the mirror reference before choosing readers to wake so a late key request can still be served from that step. The timing harness separates apply, view creation, maker builds and joins to expose serialization. It keeps marks only when the flight record is enabled and drops marks from before the observed step.

Keyed views select the requested high-word owners from the side’s whole mirror, alongside any separately declared column sets. A key discovered while settling can be answered immediately from the step’s retained mirrors. Change notices identify a whole rebuild or a list of touched keys. They are collected since the last frame the maker actually took, and are ready before a frame that uses them is published. Replacing a declaration invalidates any frame for the previous declaration. A reader that discovers undeclared content retries after demanding the full table. Tables and sets with no readers can be released.

Readers for the live picture get every relevant step. Ready-paced readers get another frame after releasing their current one. Other readers can cap their cadence and still get an immediate frame after opening, changing demand or seeking. A display reader may retain recent frames and uses the newest frame no later than the displayed hour of the current seek. Per-frame requests try the store without waiting and queue declaration changes for the side’s next post. A one-shot click or a saved frame can wait for its exact inputs within its budget. A view reader closes after a frame without demand, retaining what it learned for its next opening. A `Stepped` reader drops superseded seeks, holds newer steps until requested, and names inputs that cannot arrive once their requested hour has passed.
