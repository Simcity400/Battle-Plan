# 0311. Every widget region takes its time from one resolver and reads through one gate

A widget shows more than one time at once. Its main view reads the widget's own time, follow, campaign, a range or a pin. A drill can follow playback while the widget is pinned, or stay on an hour the user picked. A pop-out, a tooltip or a hover reads the hour under the pointer. Each of these is a **region**: a widget spec plus the user's action on it (`spec`, `follow`, or `at` a picked hour).

`dashboard/regionTime.ts` is the only place a region's hour is worked out. Axis hours (`AxisHour`, the playback clock's position) and track hours (`TrackHour`, an hour of one recorded branch) are distinct branded types at every public boundary, so one cannot be passed for the other. A track hour exists only when the branch recorded it: an hour outside the branch, or inside one of the branch's declared gaps (`BranchMetadata.gaps`, with the recording's own reason), is no hour. `coverageAt` says which, and the region's state carries the message ("outside the recording" or "not recorded") and the gap's reason. A pick keeps the hour picked, so a pick inside a gap says "not recorded" with the gap's reason. Nothing is substituted for a missing hour: no branch end, no cached hour, no parent's pin, no hour the selection was made at. A missing pin is no hour; the region says so and adds "Choose another time in widget settings." when its own pin or range put it there.

**The gate.** `dashboard/regionGate.ts` (`useRegionGate`, `useGate`) is the one production guard. It runs the stages in order, each from the recording:

1. **Coverage.** No track hour: outside, or not recorded with the gap's reason. Country groups are not even fetched.
2. **Country identity.** The selected key's holder at the region's hour, and a selection's aliases at that hour. A slot key and a tag name one country through the identity index, never by comparing strings. A failed identity read is a failed stage with a retry. A fresh choice is saved only once its holder resolves at the region's hour: never while country groups are pending or failed, nor while the user must choose between holders.
3. **Group membership**, only when the spec chooses a group or factions; Top N and every country rank instead. The selected group's membership runs and unread spans, and the identity runs of the selected countries or members, are boundaries of the region's hour, so a member joining at 1005 is present after a 1003 anchor. No other group's or country's change wakes the region.
4. **The read.**

The first stage that does not hold decides the region's state (`GateState` renders it): outside, not recorded, choose a country, choose a holder, absent at this date, no members, failed, loading. Displayed facts are dated by the reply's own hour; frame work uses the one page hour.

**Bypassing is a type error, and the transport refuses what slips past.** Exact reads take only a `GatedHour`, range bounds a `BoundHour`, which a `GatedHour` also is, so a bound can never stand in for a read. Only the gate mints either, and only while its stages hold. **Roles come from the contract, not from names.** Each native product declares, on its request type (`Product::FIELDS`, `AtHour::FIELDS`, the series and follow-head tables), the role of every request field that can carry a number, nested ones included as paths into arrays, tuples and objects (`family.membership[].firstHour`, `parts[].firstObservedHour`): an exact read hour, a range start or end, a bucket's representative hour, or none. The contract test walks each request type through serde (`fields::walk`: structs, sequences, tuples, maps through one sampled entry at `*`, options and every enum variant, each explored under the variant choices that reach it) and fails a numeric or opaque field without a role, a role on a field that is not a number, and a declared path the type does not have. A field the walk cannot enter fails the walk by name; an internally tagged enum hides its variants' fields that way, so a request type with variants deserializes through a plain struct (`try_from`) with the same JSON, and no product is exempt. The exporter writes the dated roles to `REQUEST_HOURS`, and from the same table the TypeScript parameter types: `RequestParams` per product, which `ProductRequest<K>` and `ParamsFor<K>` use when the product is known where the request is built, and `AnyRequestParams`, the top-level fields every product dates as a plain hour, for the rest (`GatedParams`). Every request is made by `productRequest`, which infers the product from its key and refuses a product name widened to `string`, or by `forwardRequest`, which takes only a declared product, and the clients and hooks take only constructed requests. The `dashboard-contract` CI job fails a checked-in contract that differs from the exporter's output. Both clients' validators use `REQUEST_HOURS` and refuse a list where the contract declares an object, or an object where it declares a list; the native side deserializes every request's params through `named`, which refuses a struct given positionally. A field's name decides nothing. An open gate gives `hour`, `window` (its start clipped to the branch, so a range or history window reaching before the branch is read from the branch's first hour, never refused for partial overlap), `hourAt(axis)` for a following read, `recorded(hour, within?)` for a hover or a reply's own hour, which also requires the selected country to hold an alias at that hour and the hour to lie in the given membership, and `within(hour)` and `span(first, last)` for range bounds inside the branch. A follow subscription is a `FollowQuery`, minted by `gate.follow` while the region follows inside its track and only when the whole query, nested params included, names no exact hour and its bounds lie inside the branch: a followed read is at playback, so `reads[]` carry countries only. A paused seek out of coverage closes the subscription and coming back reopens it. Types can be widened past, so the clients check every request at the boundary: `OpenedSources` walks every field the contract dates, nested ones included, against the opened branch and refuses by name a read or bucket hour that was not recorded, any hour outside the branch, a followed query naming a read hour, and a product the contract does not declare. Exact reads also lie inside the read country's membership: a read is sent for a country only at hours its membership covers, so a multi-country hover asks only the countries that are members of the selected group at its hour. Every `branchId` nested in a request names the request's branch, and a followed query's the subscription's: the client and the native follow registry refuse another by name, so a followed head never builds from a branch other than its playhead's. Persisted times are `GatedTime`, an opaque value made only by `validTime` (through `gate.time()` and `pinNow`), which clips each window's start to its own branch and keeps the windows whose last hour its track recorded, so the first hours of a branch pin and one short track does not void a pin of several; when no window holds, `checkedTime` names why and the settings show that refusal instead of leaving the widget live. A spread is not one, and `addWidgetBelow` takes a `PinnedSpec` carrying one and stores only its value. Pin is offered only when it would succeed: `gate.checkTime` and `gate.checkHold` give the time and holder or say why not, and a drill whose pin is refused shows the pin disabled with that reason. A dated country is persisted only from `gate.hold(holder)`: the holder is a tag at its own recorded origin, and the gate returns a `HeldCountry` (tag, origin, identity and token) only when that identity has an alias at the region's hour. Fuel, Equipment stockpile, Resource balance and the measure races (Factories, Nuclear arsenal, in their declared `holder` option) save its token; country parts, army, air loss, Stability and Equipment losses drills write their existing holder records from it, so no pin carries a tag re-read at the drill hour or a bare origin number. A restored pin resolves the holder's alias at the read hour.

**One holder identity, end to end.** A drill, pin or selection holds the country it was picked as: the pick's key and hour, a slot key such as the default player selection resolved through the identity index at the pick hour. The drill's gate resolves that holder (`CountryNeed.held`) and every read resolves the holder's dated alias at the hour it reads: the current read, a hover or exact read (`gate.country.aliasAt`), follow subscriptions, pins (`gate.held`) and history, which is read by the identity's slot within the identity's own runs (`gate.country.history`), never by the tag current at the drill hour. Keyed series take that history through one owner (`seriesOwner`, `ownerKeys` in `keyedSeries.ts`), so a part list, tile, follow and exact read of a held country all name its slot within its runs: the part catalogue is asked per run and each part's life clipped to them, so part names are read only while the identity holds the slot, and a part held across disjoint runs is listed once with each run kept apart (`heldParts`). Keyboard steps send the same `within`, and the native step never lands outside it. Production lines takes the same owner as `country` and `within`; its line keys are numbered by each line's own runs over the whole branch, so a scoped reply names a line as the full one does, and its bucket is read at the bucket's first recorded hour inside the owner's runs (`bucketRead(…, within)`). The holder's identity changes are boundaries of the drill's hour, so continuous playback across a tag change re-resolves without a seek; a slot key takes its boundaries from the same identity history its lookup reads. Two named exceptions carry hours they do not choose: the follow transport's clock and unfollow messages, and the label facts (country names, flags and styles) dated by rows already shown, whose specs return object params checked at the transport like any other.

**Drills.** Every drill uses one reducer and action set (`charts/drill/drillState.ts`) through `useDrill`, which gives the drill its own gate; every `DrillHost` takes that control and shows the drill's gate state itself. The reducer is the only place Follow, Pin and pick become a drill's time: Follow always follows, whatever the parent's mode; a saved breakdown is an open drill reading its widget's time until the user picks or follows, shown by `DrillHost` with the same day, Follow and Pin header and no Back. A drill opens following playback only under a following widget, for a recorded pick, when playing or opened near the playhead; a pick in a gap opens on that hour and says why it was not recorded. The drill's hour, reads, Follow control, day and pin come from its own gate, so a drill's data, date, state and mount never depend on its parent's hour, availability, failures or loading, and a parent leaving the track keeps the drill mounted. A drill is a full region: `DrillHost` gives the drill its own connected subscription root, and the drill computes its own chart inputs (layers, playhead, country looks, tooltip), reads, errors and playback reporting from its own replies, and its prerequisites (Production lines' bucket size) are its own reads with their own failure and retry; nothing flows from the parent except what the drill's spec inherits at open. A drill's Follow is offered whatever the parent's mode. While an inline drill is open it alone reports the widget's shown hour (`useDrillShown`): the parent's reports wait until it closes.

**No substitution.** A choice that is missing from a reply shows its unavailable state; no reader takes the first item, the branch end or another country in its place. An automatic setting the user chose, such as Resource balance's "First resource", is that choice resolved explicitly against the reply at its hour, not a stand-in for a missing one.

Playback costs nothing per frame. Region hours re-render React only when the clock is anchored (seek, step, play, pause) or a region crosses a boundary, taken from exactly the data each lookup uses and only for what the region selected: its track's ends, a declared gap (where gaps overlap, every hour a gap starts or ends, so the reason shown changes where it changes), the held or selected countries' identity runs, the selected group's membership changes, and the change hours a product declares. A product answering per bucket (Production lines' 720-hour detail windows) registers its bucket edges and is asked once per bucket at the bucket's first recorded hour, keyed by bucket, not by the frame's hour; the reply's own runs are change hours too, so the run shown changes where it changes. A following region registers the axis span over which its answer holds; the page keeps every span's ends in two heaps and a frame pops only the watches whose span it left. A region with its own fixed time registers no watch.
