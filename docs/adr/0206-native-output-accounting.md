# 0206. Record native output at the writer and account for it by branch

The game does not keep completed equipment counts per production line or daily civilian factory output consumed by each construction line. Those values come from writer occurrences. The observer pairs an equipment delivery with the credit of the same native production call on the same thread. A line blocked from deploying can credit its organisation without delivering equipment. Conversion purpose denotes conversion progress for the military producer, which does not establish that a source unit was removed. Read the identity, design, organisation and purpose at credit entry because the original can replace or refresh the line before returning. The producer commits an owned record on normal return, including an exact zero-unit tick. The production and construction censuses share the observer's line identities. The producer's raw country reference resolves through CountryLinks, and a native zero design or organisation records its absence. Unknown producer or design and an unmatched, conflicting or duplicate delivery break exactness rather than supplying a guess.

For construction, observe the configured advance's building speed read, while the railway spend comes from its first segment-progress call. Later segment calls carry existing progress between segments. Instant construction follows the viewing-country native gate because a normal railway amount can equal the instant amount. Repairs under instant construction still proceed at ordinary speed but do not withdraw their bank draw. Building speeds and the speed-plus-bank sum can be signed, and the recorder reverses the native wrapping addition before adding the bank separately. A positive bank draw is incompatible with a zero speed sum. Completion is a different observation from spending, and its slot carries the native hour or the signed quantity cleared on completion. A linked receiver can complete inside the primary advance while its output share remains unknown. The observer authenticates each spend or completion caller before reading a receiver. An unrelated removal supplies no completion.

The simulation-thread boundary hands over the settled log since its last complete owned drain. The loader and spool sizing worker prepare capacity for live demand and retained attempts. Space for two native passes allows the next pass to arrive while a previous burst waits for an owned buffer. A short consumer buffer reserves the whole pending burst before advancing its consumed frontier. Offered storage can replace the log only after a drained, writer-free boundary, and retirement is off-thread. The cursor and epoch continue across a swap. A failed observation, withdrawal or lapped producer withholds the affected interval. Counts retain every loss and the first diagnostic. An unreadable purpose stays unknown. A hook group that cannot locate and verify its required sites leaves its output unavailable by name. Construction can refuse its table after a safe rollback, while production preserves its attachment-error policy for enable failure. A rollback error survives later successful teardown so status retains the original failure.

Rocket delivery and spending use independent evidence in the shared production log. A delivery takes its producer from the acquired same-thread rocket scope, with an independently verified design. It can retain a signed unit count when the owner is inexact. Normal return resolves each occurrence. Abnormal return retains frame, module and observation ownership until teardown proves quiescence. All shared log indices stay in continuity accounting, including equipment records that add nothing to the rocket totals. The rocket table shares the equipment output schedule while remaining independent of the equipment census. A known country's refusal affects its own rows and the world aggregate. An unassignable owner withholds the world result.

The spool counts owned records by branch. The first frame's already-drained records precede its observation and do not count. It seeds completeness at the first successful census, so an ended line whose records predate that census cannot claim observation from creation. Production keeps separate totals when producer, variant, organisation or purpose changes. A known live line with no production has a zero row, while missing or overflowed values remain withheld. Scheduled output publishes with the census and off-slot hours carry the previous publication. An admitted loss withholds the table from that hour even off-slot. An already-ended line stays in publications until a written frame holds its end. Each receipt acknowledges its own publication only. An old branch or a reused handle cannot retire new output, and later output under an ended handle remains until its own publication is written.

Synthetic observer tests hold the process-global lock while resetting their observation cursor and using owned native-shaped inputs. Tests can trip receiver validation with an invalid address for a caller that should be ignored. Threaded cases exercise producers claiming distinct log indices. The release-only construction benchmark measures guarded reads, atomic appends and drains from fixtures, separate from native detour and simulation time.

## Log and bridge ownership

Construction `log` can borrow the active allocation because writers hold `OBSERVATION.enter()` and the sole worker drain finishes before the parked boundary can install a replacement. The runtime joins those drains and restores the hook family before releasing its storage. Equipment `log` uses the producer's `Call` guard and the simulation thread as its sole consumer. Only that consumer's empty, writer-free boundary can replace the log. Detach drains every callback before releasing storage. The output hook constructors resolve every site and trampoline before publication and register the native flight and callback ranges when the family needs them.

Construction's `speed_entry` uses the unused third argument register to pass the caller's return address to the Rust observer and leaves the stack untouched. `building_complete_entry` uses the second argument register for that address and returns the original low byte. `linked_complete_entry` receives two native arguments and passes the caller's return address in the third register. Its observation precedes the deletion of the linked receiver. Callers outside the identified advance do not justify receiver reads or completion records. The equipment helper `live_u64` is used while the native caller still owns the line and variant it supplied.

Rocket `owner_entry!` keeps its observer call's stack arguments, saved native input registers, saved integer and floating return values, flight ownership and `Context` in separate regions of its frame. `CONTEXT` begins after those call, save and ownership slots. The original native call is made outside Rust frames. The assembly prologue describes the actual frame in its unwind metadata so a native escape retains the correct ownership.
