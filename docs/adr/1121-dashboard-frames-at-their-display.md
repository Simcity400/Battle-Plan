# 1121. Draw each dashboard frame for the display it reaches, and never decode a product's table on the stepper

A drawing worker's frame reaches the screen no sooner than the first vsync after its work begins. The worker reads the clock at that moment and draws its hour. Pictures, page notices and the date face carry the moment, and page guards compare a notice's head with the clock at the same moment. The worker measures the display interval from its animation frames whenever it plays, assuming 60 Hz until it has measured one. The moment is a lower bound. A frame that misses its vsync shows an hour behind the clock, never one ahead. The playback check holds a picture only from the first presented display at or after that moment, because a display composed before the picture existed cannot show it.

Only the dashboard playhead's stepper runs time-critical. Its step helpers and its share of a seek run at normal priority, and fetching runs below normal. A product's step reads its tables from the step's lanes. If the step lacks a lane that the host will give it, the reader looks among the placed tables the product pool holds near the hour and steps one forward. If none is near, the reader places the table on its own thread and reports the read pending. A pending read confirms no hour, so the page keeps its last picture while the rest of the step goes on. The host sends that product whole at the next step. A seed has no step, so it reads directly and leaves its tables placed, and the step after it can use them at once. A table that cannot be placed is read directly, as before.

A product's live values are current only when it builds a reply or a caller asks for them. A seed, a resend and a complete read ask, and the reader fills them at that hour without rebuilding the reply. A streamed reply that only carried moving values keeps its data, and the page reads those values through its followed cells. An advance that only reads the held reply leaves it shared, and one that writes it copies it first.
