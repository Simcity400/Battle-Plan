# 0802. Derive air views from their individual observation boundaries

The recorder derives independent tables for the air force, mission roster, regional activity and loss ledger. Each table uses its own observed inputs. Lost equipment coverage withholds wing composition while leaving whole-plane and wing totals available. A failed registration join withholds that hour's mission roster, while wings by mission region still come straight from observed wing rows. A theatre filter uses changes to the theatre's region rows so the reader can avoid scanning the theatre tables at every hour.

## Force and mission attribution

Each wing counts for its roster owner and effective country. A positive override supplies the effective owner. Every live country has a totals row. An unresolved owner withholds that attribution for all countries, and a negative plane word withholds the affected country's planes while preserving its wing count. The composition writer records equipment counted in fixed-five units. A positive whole-plane quantity counts under its variant and the remaining recorded planes under the unnamed part. Invalid splits keep the total under the unnamed part and leave variant totals unknown. A composition signature consists of its positive-quantity variants in ascending order. The composition writer assigns each live signature a run-unique number and retires it after its last wing leaves. A signature that returns takes a new number.

Mission registrations join wings to owner slots and their mission regions. Each registered wing contributes in its registration order. Only positive masks select mission bits. The per-bit plane arithmetic wraps like the game's arithmetic and restarts a negative sum from zero before adding the next wing. The separate membership rows preserve ordinal order for range reads in a regional drill-down. One failed join withholds both mission tables, with their last rows held until a complete read can replace them. Any wing with a mission region contributes to the independent all-owner counts. A theatre counts a wing where its group's theatre and wing region are known, and loses the count when the wing, membership, theatre or region goes away.

## Ledger and timeline

The loss and bombing totals count appended events at the same available reads as the ledger. A complete or truncated log can count when the country links are available. A read whose inputs are unavailable adds nothing. Identity resolves at the read that counts an event. Losses group by region and native cause and track whether the opposing country resolved. Aircraft-call bombing adds output to the attacker's dealt and victim's taken columns. A total that exceeds the signed 64-bit representation stays unrecorded. The largest difference between event and read hour records lateness, including captured backlog. Reading an interval can use these accumulated totals instead of scanning earlier events.

Air owner scores and aggregate region activity keep cumulative last-seen daily scores. Their rows change when scores change and can be read at two endpoints to get a span. Negative scores and vanished score entries contribute zero under the game's score arithmetic. The viewed side composition uses the leaders, faction membership and war pairs in the recorded hour. It resolves each owner through link and tag-holder state using the same selection as the moment's composition. A slot with multiple tags uses its last tag, and duplicate score owners resolve to the greatest raw id at that region. A faction side reads its leader's score and a lone country reads its own. An owner with a negative score contributes no pair, and a time without known group history contributes no composed side activity. These tables retain historic totals after score entries vanish.

At a fresh hour or after resume, the writers reread their source tables and recreate indexes before applying new changes. Changes to links, holders and group context invalidate the corresponding derived state. The air force composition writer persists the next signature number, while the other air writers can reconstruct their in-memory state from the tables.
