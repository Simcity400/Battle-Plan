# Admit event feed writers in short leases and assign declines to the correct seal

The event feed holds its store for checked acquisition or a merge, releasing it before native execution. Worker recovery and sealing measure demand and prepare storage outside the lease. A merge replaces backing allocation only when no ticket can point into it. Each owner seals serially. A worker that loses a race for the free store is owed the next release, so callbacks decline while it waits. The only callback that may wait is Candidate publication behind another Candidate publication. The latter copies prepared operands and cannot call native code, reenter the observer or wait for the game thread. A parked Candidate joins no decline phase. It retries admission with the live ownership word and writer cut after wakeup. It registers interest against the exact holder so a release before it parks cannot be missed, and checks for terminal retention even if the holder never releases. A store admission decline records a missed observation but leaves the native call and its flight intact. Settlement of an existing flight is independent of ordinary missed observation accounting.

Every attempt to enter the store and both ends of a forwarded native writer move the writer cut. A baseline requires a stable cut and no bracketed call in flight. One decline accounting phase corresponds to each seal. Before attempting entry, the callback increments an entering count and validates that the phase is still current. The seal flips that phase as its last action while holding the lease. It releases and waits only for attempts that validated the previous phase. They finish with a bounded set of atomic operations. Sequential consistency makes their increments visible to this drain and moves a late join into the successor phase. Exactly one consumer claims each decline range. Misses during the closing phase refuse the row just sealed, and their store effects apply on the next lease. A lost drain with no publication invalidates the next interval. New entrants take losses in the current phase only, so they cannot steal a closed phase's declines from the seal. A parked callback never holds a phase open.

Receipt storage is reserved per seal. Owner serials prevent an old owner's receipt from applying to a replacement at the same address. Only the first outcome for a pending publication counts, and the writer acknowledges after a real successful write. Declines and storage shortages remain named refusals even if they occur after measured sizing or while a seal holds the lease. Every required provenance forwarder must remain observed for interval coverage to be complete.

The observer-only native guard leaves an original exception free to propagate. An uncertain native or callback escape retains ownership permanently, including any flight and the storage it may reference. The fault sink records this state without touching payloads or inventing ticket settlement. The observer revokes coverage before an unobserved mutation, leaving subsequent observation to establish the new world. Ownership and receipt records are pointer-free for reporting. Production never resets a retained flight or terminal fault across load, gap or recovery.

The observer keeps recovery scopes for decision and delayed registries independent. Each owner transaction copies a complete baseline privately on a worker, then rechecks its cut at publication before beginning the next registry. A partial census or a loss awaiting its receipt cannot certify an owner. Facts that remain unacknowledged and the local refusal reserve count toward measured capacity. Seal prepares its publication receipt before the output leaves the boundary. A forwarded owner-retirement pass releases its authenticated cursor only after the original returns; a destroy without that pass cannot forward its ownership.

The native bridge acquires one flight at entry and retires it only on normal exit. C++ holds opaque ticket storage, and only Rust consumes or grants a settlement token. Slot admission and receipt identity use owner serials rather than a pointer that can be reused. Callback latches stage witnesses by frame token and epoch. A missed entry or reused arena epoch produces a named interval refusal rather than claiming a complete sequence. The observer brackets originals that have no after-store callback and forwards only the operand reached by a provenance writer.

The aligned `FLIGHT` word remains live for assembly. Its low bits count native flights and its high bit makes retention irreversible. Only assembly entry and normal exit modify the flight count. `wait_candidate` compares the live aligned ownership word with a same-sized temporary. It rechecks terminal retention on timeout or wake because an escape can preserve the busy word forever. `Owner::held` wakes that same word while its enclosing native flight and synchronous callback still guarantee the owner lifetime. The C++ bridge owns the stack frame passed to `hoi4_event_before`. `returned` marks the ticket consumed before reading it, so a failed settlement cannot reuse it.

`Hooks::set_enabled` keeps the delayed observer installed while any native flight can still use its cursor anchor. Its drop path also releases unpublished bootstrap storage. The test guards restore the shared owner before dropping their private replacement and expose the test-only store only while its lease is clear.
