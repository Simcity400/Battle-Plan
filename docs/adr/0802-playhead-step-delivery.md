# 0802. Deliver shared playback steps without blocking readers

The stepper owns playback lanes and publishes their owned changes to a shared ring. Fetch threads read the recording, rebuild requested reader state after a seek and dispose of retired values. Reader frames use nonblocking access to the ring and control state and retry on a later frame if a lock is held. The first reader taking a seek reset updates the clock at the searched hour before that frame draws. Subsequent readers can take the same reset independently.

The stepper builds toward the furthest reader's frame head. Work predicted to take long enough is split among step threads, with the largest lanes offered first. Seek and join jobs borrow threads while the machine has capacity, and fetchers prioritize reads that active steps need. A seek's clock remains held at the target until its reset is taken. Queued and completed reads carry an epoch so failures from an abandoned seek or retired declaration cannot stop the active stepper. Log reads requested by ordinal use the independent log cursor and take their place after reads required for active steps.

The ring bounds how far it can build beyond both the clock head and the furthest consumer. A lead adds a byte limit but still permits one step at the head. Each reader can hold a shared step until it consumes the step and releases its reference. Steps for one reader are delivered in sequence with shared steps. An ahead look does not take a step. An ahead look can keep an old immutable step while the ring publishes revised change sets for consumers that have not taken it. Released buffers can be reused or freed away from the stepper so allocation and freeing do not follow each frame's workload.

A lagging consumer keeps shared steps while their payload fits within the size of reconstructing its held lane state. The ring can park a consumer beyond that bound, retaining its earlier position and waiting for a fresh landing. Consumers that never supplied a frame head, request their own landings, hold a private step, stand before a reset or lack complete tables do not take that eviction path. A seek abandons the prior epoch, including individual steps and in-flight requests. A pace change can rebuild untaken steps and invalidate their earlier ahead views. A gap carries no recorded changes and retains the prior piece's last rows until crossing to the next piece.

Reader declarations select a union of lanes and columns. A table already held by the stepper can supply an individual reset to a moving reader from memory. A reader standing short of a previously prepared landing can get a file read at its own hour even while another reader moves. When a new lane is required, the old lane continues stepping. Once the replacement can cover the newest step, it supplies the declaring reader's state and replaces its column sets in future steps without resetting other readers. A reader requesting several tables in one declaration gets them together. Redelivering a table after a reader retired it requires a reset even if the union has not changed. A no-join declaration can reach the hook without the clock advancing.

Parked readers of logs or rows that can appear and disappear within a step catch up from the recording to preserve events that the held state alone cannot reconstruct. A catch-up ends at each lane's actual prior position, including a short position, so the shared steps continue it exactly. For a smaller playback quantum, the stepper seeks lanes to the last taken step and rebuilds only later steps. The hook suppresses repeat publication for hours it already saw. A larger quantum keeps shorter built steps. At piece boundaries the stepper delivers a delta between the adjacent lane states. Log ordinals and string ids belong to a run, so a log resets and reused text ids still get rewritten when their spelling changes. Pending declaration changes invalidate only affected preselected lanes for the next piece.

Hourly reader selections attach per-hour values for hours that wrote the table, in addition to an aggregate step. A catch-up, reset, piece crossing or step spanning a lane's accumulated shortage publishes its aggregate span. Status, text, passing rows and renumbering live on the aggregate. A reader rebuild takes its selected columns from the sought lane. The reset holds the rebuild until that reader takes it. The playhead tracks the bytes and count still in flight, preserving peaks separately.
