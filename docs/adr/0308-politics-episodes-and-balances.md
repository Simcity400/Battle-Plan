# 0308. Build political episodes and balances from dated recorded operands

Political series take recorded histories per stretch and name holders through the common identity rules. Election eligibility requires a holder owning states and the recorded eligibility predicate. An unavailable predicate and a known negative verdict differ. A date requires the recorded calendar inputs, including the signed interval. A gap or a change of holder ends a state run. A recorded raw id change can still belong to the same canonical holder, whose known pieces are sorted by hour before joining.

Civil war episodes connect listings through a shared holder while their intervals meet or overlap. They keep every distinct side. A side comes from its earliest listed rival already participating when the side first appears. The game still considers the war active only for a listing where at least one of its two holders owns a state. Stale listings can extend a side without extending the active war. A winner requires every other side to have been annexed by a participating side. A recorded restoration contributes a later life and invalidates an earlier exit as the final one. The annexation must name a side already present at its own hour to count as an exit from that war.

Balance positions identify records only within a recorded hour. The game's chosen balance is the first record listing the holder and requires resolved membership. The history reads relevant records per stretch. The state and history products resolve names, ranges and sides through the recording's content. A known numeric value stays visible if content withholds a name or range. Moving quantities use live cells. Drift sources are named in recorded static-modifier order, separately from members' contributions. The breakdown requires a name for every source.
