# Viewer publications follow the simulation and hand off whole pictures

## Clock and ownership

A playhead entry is shared by readers of the same recording, timeline, side and clock mapping. The viewer and followed dashboard queries use different sides so a query does not contend with the stepper that supplies frames. A later map joins the existing clock and declares its own tables. It neither resets the first map nor chooses the hour from which its tables join. A reader's open and disposal happen on its worker. A running map keeps presenting its previous products while an open or reset is in flight.

Set pace before seeking because pace determines how steps built ahead of the head are sized. A pause within a built step needs an exact seek. The side prepares a publication at the simulation rate, with a lag estimate derived from how long the previous publication took to reach a display frame. A moving head generally needs a new hour before a due publication. A stationary head still answers view changes and carries. A viewing-country change may publish at frame pace to reduce time to the new perspective. The stepper and lanes it waits on retain sufficient scheduling priority. Background fetches cannot hold a display frame. A declaration can require fresh rows and a compose, including when a layer's own requested columns change but its mirror already holds them for a maker.

One side thread owns the mirrors and layer state for each map. The frame posts its latest view and head without holding the side lock. During ordinary playback it tries to take a brief window to write scene uploads and take shared products. If the side is busy, the frame reuses the last products. The frame releases the window to signal that the next publication can begin. The worker bounds the wait for a latest-wins message, because a wake for a message posted outside the lock can arrive between checking and waiting. Diagnostic readers can lock the side, but the drawing path uses a nonblocking read. A separate reader of the hour can open on the side's published view store without taking this lock.

## Compose and settle

The host applies each step to its mirrors before running its layers. A whole reset moves the mirrors and layers to a worker. Layers that share dependencies compose in one group in registration order, since a layer is `Send` and may be accessed by only one compose thread at a time. The groups are scheduled longest-first using their previous durations. The reset's frame shows the composed picture at the sought hour before applying later steps. A superseded compose remains unshown and is replaced by the newer reset. With one map, later steps can remain in the ring while composing. With other maps on the same playhead, each composing map continues consuming into its backlog so the shared ring does not stall. At a piece crossing, only the layers whose mirrors reset or renumber compose synchronously before the other layers settle.

Maker views posted by a step finish reading the mirrors before the next write. Layers that depend on a maker or on another layer that joins one settle once their upstream products can be taken. Ordinary paced publications take ready products and wait at a layer's lag bound to avoid starvation. A stationary head waits for all work it owes, since there may be no later frame that could show it. A capability refusal names the affected layer. A layer that awaits a newly declared table composes only when every required mirror has landed. Visibility or placement changes can invalidate a layer's declaration. Camera-only changes use a placement check before they re-declare the whole view.

One publication can carry multiple game hours. The side folds the interval once and dates each timed change from the step's hourly rows. Mover paths use the original slots before the step reuses or renumbers them. The display owns timed-layer installers, which write through each frame's hour even while its side is busy. A timed layer cannot depend on another layer or provide input to it in the same settle graph, because those products can differ in hour. The reported displayed hour accounts for both the oldest untimed product that holds the picture and the timed installers' coverage. A cadence product can be older without holding that clock, but it still counts when reporting the oldest thing on screen. While a mode or perspective is being established, the displayed picture keeps its settled mode. A mismatched or refused perspective withdraws the overlay and dependent parts rather than showing another country's data. The overlay can stand through an ordinary seek while the next vision is unknown.

## Exact stepped frames

A stepped ask identifies the hour and its entire requested picture, including its view, overlay placement, counters and combat window. Its serial distinguishes even two asks at the same hour. The side catches up without waiting for unshown intermediate pictures and composes the requested picture whole. The frame waits only for that ask within the capture deadline. A refusal answers that one capture, gives its reason, keeps older handed products available, and leaves a later ask free to try again. The side owns the catch-up, so a stepped capture never waits for a frame-driven operation it is itself preventing. Timed layers must also install the target hour before it is drawn. The saved picture gives the same hour's overlay and combat data, or is refused by name.
