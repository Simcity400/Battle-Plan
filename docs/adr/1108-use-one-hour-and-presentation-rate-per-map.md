# Use one hour and presentation rate per map

The playback clock owns the replay position. A live frame uses one head for mover placement, timed layers and the reported hour. The simulation can publish ahead of the clock. When publication runs late, steer the head by at most one eighth of the playback rate, targeting one eighth of a publication interval below the newest available hour and at most one interval behind the clock. The head continues advancing so the picture avoids a visible stall and catch-up jump. If the head outruns available placement, the reported hour stops at the newest publication, where the actual positions and layer data also stop. When nothing has published, target the current clock. Pauses, seeks, reversals and saved frames take the clock directly. A speed change supplies the new clock advance immediately. A seek or branch change clears the old history.

Absolute replay hours exceed the range at which a float can represent consecutive hours. The frame keeps an integer hour and a separate fraction. Mover tracks use the recording's positions for the hours a publication can cover. A route preview or travel estimate is unsuitable for interpolating the picture because it can diverge from recorded movement and snap back on the next publication. Interpolate between recorded neighbours, hold a track's endpoint beyond its range, and unwrap each segment against the adjacent point before folding it onto the map. Different tracks at the same place keep separate identities.

Every map budgets against its effective display rate. The fastest participating display caps the shared simulation rate. A valid app limit can lower both rates. Validate the configured limit at the protocol and harness boundaries. Each map owns a deadline grid advanced from the last scheduled deadline. A frame at least a full interval late restarts the grid so the next frame does not race to catch up.

The live budget learns the work following each mark and subtracts it and the current frame's elapsed time from the refresh interval. The learning step follows one tenth of each difference to absorb a single late frame. Its guard finalizes observations at drop, including after an early return. A piece may defer for three frames and then must finish. A saved frame, stepped capture or offline dump must be whole, so it bypasses budgeting. The budget and hour checks reconcile per-part work against these promises, including pending work when playback stops and layer changes installed outside their recorded hour.
