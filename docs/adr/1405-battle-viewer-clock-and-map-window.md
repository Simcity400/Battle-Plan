# 1405. The battle viewer: the game's battle window on the battle's own clock, and a map inside a window

## Context

The dashboard and its maps share one playback clock. The battle viewer plays one battle from its first hour to its last with its own play button and scrubber, in a window over the dashboard, with the app's native map of the battle's place filling the window's body.

Native maps are drawn by one child window that sits above the page. Its shape is the union of the shown maps' rectangles less the rectangles of open popovers ("holes"), and a map that a hole covers entirely is not drawn. A map inside a popover window would therefore be cut out by its own window.

## Decision

- **The battle window first.** The viewer opens on the game's own battle window, the one the game shows when a battle on its map is clicked, replayed at the clock's hour. The native map host draws it as a battle surface, a view with no scene: the viewer's combat window bound from the recorded rows of that hour, with the interface and art of the current installation through the recorded mod stack, presented on the GPU the way a map view is. It uses the recording's map content that the dashboard's maps hold and the icon art already uploaded for their scenes; it uploads its own copy of that art only when no scene on the device holds it. Its hourly reads run on a worker with a cursor that steps forward hour by hour and decodes only the tables the window has asked for. Everything it holds goes when it closes. The game shows a battle window only to a country engaged in it, so the window opens as the battle's first recorded attacker, the same country for every hour, with fog of war on; at an hour that country is not engaged, the view says so. After the battle's last hour the game has no window; the view says when the battle ended and its result. The map of the battle's place and the course charts are further views of the same window.
- **Pause, then resume.** While a battle viewer is open the dashboard's clock is paused; closing the viewer resumes it if the viewer paused it. The rest of the dashboard holds the hour it was at. This is the owner's signed-off exception to the one shared clock.
- **The battle's clock.** The viewer's clock runs from the battle's first hour to its last, or to the dashboard's hour for a battle still going on. It plays a whole battle in about twelve seconds, at least one game hour and at most one game day a second, so a long battle plays slower and every hour is drawn. The battle surface is re-targeted at every hour the clock passes. The map view sends its own hour, play state and speed, so the native map interpolates between hours on its own path, and the page only re-targets the map on play, pause and seek.
- **Map windows.** An element registered as a map surface is marked `data-map-surface`. An open popover's hole leaves out the map surfaces inside it and inside any popover opened above it; tooltips, menus and windows opened later still cut those maps. While a popover holds a map surface on the shared playhead, every map outside such a popover is left out of the views sent to the native map, and it comes back when the popover closes. Maps of one recording share one playhead, so a dashboard map that stayed loaded but hidden would keep the window's map from presenting its own hour; leaving it out releases its scene and reader. A battle surface reads on its own cursor, not the playhead, and leaves the maps outside it loaded. A popover that dims the page behind it (its backdrop is drawn) hides the maps outside it while it is open, so no map draws over the dimmed page; they stay loaded. The rules name no widget.
- **Focus province.** A map view may name a province. The viewer moves its camera to that province's centre from the recording's own map layout, with the same move the map's search box makes, once for each new province; the user is then free to pan and zoom.

## Consequences

- The Map view builds a map view of its own, a whole scene for the same recording.
- A dashboard map behind an open battle viewer is hidden while it is open, and, like any map that is not drawn, closes its reader. When the viewer closes from the Battle or Details view the map keeps its scene and reopens its reader at the dashboard's hour, under a second on the vanilla recording. After the Map view it also rebuilds its scene, under two seconds.
- While the battle plays, the battle surface reads every hour in turn on its cursor, a step forward from the hour before; a forward jump of up to 32 hours steps through the hours between, which costs less than seeking, and a longer or backward jump seeks. The surface holds the window's game layout at 1920×1080 while it is open, sharing a layout for an equivalent size that the maps already hold, and lets it go on close; the picture is scaled to the space the viewer gives it.
