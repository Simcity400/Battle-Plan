# 1119. Separate map presentation from the host clock and content lifecycle

The host sends clock anchors and the map advances a running clock locally. Anchor age is measured on the page's clock with the offset learned from its anchors. A running position advances for delivery delay and cannot jump back when a late anchor, a rate change, or a resume arrives. Seeks, pauses, and branch changes take their exact specified position. Playback clamps at the branch end. A seek made while the reader is opening or closed stays owed until the reader takes it. A reader over another branch or content revision cannot satisfy the map. A hidden map releases its reader so it cannot hold the shared playhead's ring for other maps.

The frame takes one head, kept within the recorded range the publications provide, and places the scene's moving parts at the hour its timed layers actually show. The side may have decoded newer rows. Window readers are bounded by the presented branch and hour, and after a seek they cannot read the history it left. A playing frame can show an earlier overlay from the same seek while newer layers advance. Still pictures and saved frames wait for the exact hour, including the overlay. The map's first picture stays behind the spinner until its required layers and overlay stand together. A refusal of the viewing country's vision withholds the dependent products and names the reason. It does not hold the picture for an overlay that will never come. Until a new mode or perspective has a picture, the visible picture's mode and perspective still own what is drawn and read. A seek or reopened reader resets local movement and lighting histories, and recreated units also reset audio continuity.

The window takes the map side's prepared products without waiting on an interactive frame. Timed layer changes already handed over can be installed while the side is busy. A paused seek from an error view keeps requesting frames until it has an answer. The host gets the hour and settings the render actually used, not the requested values. Data limitations are replaced with each frame, with current hover and combat gaps above persistent content notes. The performance and status reports distinguish a picture behind the clock from one of a different history or later than the clock, which cannot serve as its answer.

An active load and folder retries take precedence over an old accepted map. A host seek names the recording and its revision in addition to its branch and hour, and an unverified native-picker revision cannot satisfy it. A host open coalesces older work. A seek's explicit snapshot supersedes queued opens but does not route to a different accepted file that happens to share its hour. A loader that finishes after a newer choice is discarded. An embedded viewer can wait for folders while holding its request; a standalone viewer names the refusal. Loading builds reusable, hour-independent GPU work away from the render thread while the first hour prepares. Resizing past the resource budget retires the view's texture. A new scene can start at the same generation number, so reuse also requires a live registration.

Campaign and dashboard maps share verified content, the host's worker pool, and one visual fallback owner per recording and preference key. A preference reload preserves the camera, free-mode start and running clock. The host does not send another anchor on reload or after showing the map, so the map catches up from its retained clock. A mod refresh requested during a load checks again once the load is installed, in case the loader mounted a revision before files changed. Reload stale dashboard content before the Campaign map can reuse it. A failed scene with visual mods can probe the recording's own content. Maps feed the owner named refusals and the settled frame status, and reload if their content no longer matches its decision. The current game art is resolved through the active content, while recorded facts and executable tables come from the recording.

An interactive view claims process audio only after it has presented a picture. A dashboard gives one map at a time that focus. A hidden view, error screen, or spinner releases its audio so the next successful presentation starts its sounds afresh. Captures do not open an audio device. A diagnostic run can still log what it would play. Active controls send ordered one-shot interface sounds through the same process owner. A repeated click on one control can replace its earlier, unstarted voice, without interrupting a one-shot already playing. View volume, mute and the game's master volume apply even when no frame is presented.
