# 1203. Treat subject relations as dated native identities

Group each relation by both native endpoints. Split when either endpoint's recorded holder changes, even where the same printed tag survives. A change of tag alone keeps one relation, and a later country reusing a tag has its own lanes. Use structured lane ids and treat recorded relation starts as evidence that distinguishes a rereleased subject from an ongoing relation. Only use the legacy path without a native catalogue. Native endpoint attribution gaps remain unknown. A literal tag choice may include an earlier holder. Group membership can restrict that holder to recorded membership dates.

When separate runs identify the same relation by start time, bridge the gap as continuity with an unknown level. An observation of no relation can bound its end. A missing read does not show a release, and neither unrecorded counts nor unresolved endpoints become zero. Show former subjects and their overlords after relations have ended, clipping to known starts before the playback hour. Resolve endpoint names at the shown or hovered hour, including a tag change after release. Keep native proof gaps in the label rather than falling back to a nearby run's printed tag.

The lane layout follows relation and level changes, unrecorded spans and alias changes. It uses the recorded autonomy-state content order for labels and palette colors, including modded levels. Colors past the palette share an Other category. A missing level shows as uncolored, while a known level whose localization is unavailable gets a readable fallback. The neutral overlord band uses the count on recorded intervals. Expose the same moment's list to keyboard and screen-reader users, and keep the legend for a single level since the color conveys its identity.
