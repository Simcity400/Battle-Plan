# 0903. Keep strategic air sides anchored to historical participation

The recording has bilateral war participation. Distinct shared enemies establish that groups fight alongside one another. Merge those allied groups before placing lone countries or reading scores, and prevent a merge when any members are directly hostile. An enemy of a lone country already assigned to a side cannot join it. Lone-country ties count the enemies a side leader also fights and then take the numeric faction anchor. A faction's first eligible leader follows recorded order. Duplicate faction membership cannot supply a reliable partition, and an unobserved roster or unresolved member withholds assignment.

Side identity follows historical ally components and earliest recorded war participation, with numeric ID or tag as the deterministic tie-breaker. A later low-ID ally cannot displace an established anchor. When an anchor is present, the historical identity stays with its side. If the anchor leaves, the surviving earliest participant takes it, which lets a lone remnant keep the same key. Indexed interval queries read only group runs active at the selected hour. The hostility index maintains neighbours on current component roots so a failed merge is cheap, and a successful merge only moves the smaller component's neighbours.

Composition stores the identity of a supplying row instead of baking a score into the derived cell. The recorded region owns the value. A read distinguishes unavailable data, a country without a strategic-air object and known zero. A side with a leader demands the leader's scores. Leaderless groups consider their members. A cell carries each scoring side's place, its supplying country and that country's raw row key. The fallback and region cells use stable roster places. The recorder's daily composition and the dashboard share the same selection logic.
