# Derive naval layers from recorded rows before camera placement

Naval control tracks supremacy per strategic region and computes its relation to the viewing country. The political fill supplies these attributes to the water at the appropriate hour. A local supremacy edit dirties its region. Country links and blockade tuples can affect all regions. Other relation inputs are tracked by what the cached sides read, and changed facts invalidate the affected classifications. A change from one truncation count to another can update the hour's warning without reclassifying for a status change. Hourly visibility packets are valid only for their own hour. Positive receivers derive directed wars from complete relation rows and refuse self-links, missing links, or links to the reserved receiver. Supported older recordings use their recorded directed-war packets.

Camera grouping remains separate from the recorded naval counter inputs. The counter layer keeps task-force membership, fleet heads, ship inputs, and naval base state. Offscreen task forces can defer recompute until admitted. A naval base can need its docked counters regardless of the camera. Ship-based ratios require complete censuses and model inputs. A missing base census or a named but undecided task force can leave its port unresolved. Peace-conference visibility and viewing-country relations invalidate the members that depend on them. Refusing or hiding the layer discards retained work, since its tables may be released until it next composes.

Naval combat markers rebuild together because spill-over and transfers can move icons outside their combat's own province. The layer limits invalidation to watched rows and columns, and reads engaged flags from the prior layer. The retained frame index identifies which combat history changed over a step. An index provenance change rereads all live frames, while an unchanged hour reads none. The comparison tool uses the same history and perspective. Its counter proof names the operands it does not compare.
