# 1106. Fold combat engagement and icon frames from recorded hours

The viewer reconstructs engagement from the recording for the selected viewing country. A primary country is engaged if it is the viewer or shares vision with it, and a roster owner qualifies if it is the viewer. Shared vision checks war first, then faction membership and subject or overlord relationships in either direction. A proven positive stands even if another reference is unresolved. An unresolved reference can prevent a negative, so incomplete evidence produces an unknown flag. Diplomacy changes alone leave the previously rebuilt flag held.

On load all sides rebuild against the loaded sample. Between samples a changed viewing country proves an all-side rebuild when witness coverage is unavailable or on a hypothetical perspective. From format 146 onward, `CombatEngagementRefreshes` drains sparse hourly witnesses of every native refresh call that can leave hourly rosters unchanged: unit additions and removals on land, border-war, and naval sides (each carrying the execution viewing country, or zero when observing no country), naval convoy transfer additions, controller-reassignment command targets, and local all-combat refreshes (carrying the local sweep's execution viewing country, or zero when observing no country). When `CombatEngagementRefreshes` is complete, its per-side witnesses prove `Recorded` or `Units` refreshes directly (including same-roster re-adds, remove-and-readd bounces, mid-hour convoy add-and-sinks, and disambiguating one-sided land round retreats), controller targets prove an all-side refresh on hypothetical perspectives (unresolved or remapped targets leave a possible all-side refresh), and the recording client track evaluates both local all-combat sweeps and witnessed side refreshes under their recorded execution viewpoints without inferring an all-side sweep from sampled observer lag alone or synthesizing unwitnessed unit removals when a previous census was partial. An execution viewpoint of zero evaluates directed shared vision with the reserved country from `CountryCounterInputs`, `ReservedCountryNaming`, and `FactionMembers` (returning unknown whenever those operands are unavailable); recording-track roster checks preserve raw viewing tags so secondary owners match by raw tag equality (allowing slot equality only when both tags are nonzero) and primary owners resolving to the reserved country use directed reverse shared vision rather than slot equality. When `CombatEngagementRefreshes` is unavailable, truncated, or not recorded (formats before 146), unchanged hourly rosters cannot prove no refresh ran, so every side carries a possible refresh (and controller rosters fall back to `Players`/`HumanControllers` join heuristics): steady diplomacy stays definite while changed diplomacy becomes ambiguous rather than asserting a stale held flag. Joins append owners and test the recorded lists. Removals rebuild attacker then defender from remaining units, even when the removed unit was absent from a side. A border war start refreshes its side. Combat absence proves it started or ended only with a whole census.

The fold evaluates the allowed list sources against relations at both sampled times, each paired with its own country links. A country switch or reference remapping must be evaluated using the links at the time of the possible refresh. The held flag remains a candidate when no refresh was proven. An unresolved candidate makes the result unknown. Conflicting evaluations give an order ambiguity, while a conflict only with the held flag gives a scope ambiguity. A gap in the hours clears eligibility to carry a known flag. A load starts a fresh history for convoy-slot observations.

The history indexes both the chosen perspective and the recording’s own player. The latter track takes precedence for that player and can settle undecided naval flags from the recorded bytes. The index stores ordered change points and per-hour combat IDs together, so advancing several hours can update exactly the affected combats. A change of viewer invalidates a delta across perspectives. A lookup outside a covered track can build a load-style flag with named provenance. A saved frame waits on publication up to its limit when the index is still pending. A terminal mismatch, an unavailable viewer or an uncaptured index answers without that wait.

An icon lives while both side lists are nonempty. Its seen update sets its frame and subsequent unseen hours keep that frame for the same instance. Released pool slots can retain an old frame, and the native camera can cause release when a province leaves view or cutoff or its projection fails. The recording lacks the camera and pool state, so the fold assumes a camera that kept watching and reports that assumption on carried frames. If a side may have emptied and refilled or an hour could not establish the icon’s existence, visibility or frame, the carried observation cannot be used. A land fallback uses the authored initial frame, defaulting to frame one. A naval fallback explicitly sets frame one because the binding overrides the authored frame. The visible results retain the limitation of the history state that led to that presentation choice.

Table access in the fold can use a decoded frame or retained table lanes and distinguishes complete, truncated, unavailable and unrecorded data. Perspective resolution matches tags case-insensitively and uses the recorded viewpoint, including its observer fallback, if the requested country is absent, then the lowest canonical slot. A missing country never becomes an invented viewpoint. The land marker mirrors progress when its viewing country shares vision with either defender set. The map uses current-hour inputs and counts reserves when summing ordered member organisation. The progress refusal names the first missing member in attacker-then-defender order. A province combat picture needs one engaged vector entry and a progress size needs two. An incomplete vector or ambiguous flag with an effect on the threshold withholds the result.
