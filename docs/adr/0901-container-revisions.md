# 0901. Publish columnar revisions with a durable footer

The recording container appends a revision's extents and syncs them, writes and syncs its resume slot, appends and syncs the root, and then appends and syncs its footer. The footer commits the root. `commit_with` calls its preparation callback after the root is durable and before the footer. Recovery can still reach either committed revision if that operation is interrupted. An interrupted write leaves the unfinished tail beyond the committed footer. The writer can carry an earlier resume slot in a revision that leaves owner state intact. On finalization it embeds per-family final state, commits a root without a resume slot, and removes the state sidecar.

Readers open the newest saved revision and writers can open the latest one even while it is unsaved. An unsaved root points to the prior saved revision by number, length through the footer, and root digest. The reader validates that reference and reports a broken reference. When no saved root exists the file has no saved revision to open. Saving appends a root, and discarding truncates to the saved revision's length. Revision numbers increase, including across file replacement. Sections and resume slots are checked against their owning schema versions. Readers report a version mismatch by section name, and an unknown section kind makes the root unreadable.

The state sidecar's two headers each seal the slot sequence, offset, length, and payload digest. A new payload is placed clear of the slot named by the previous root. Sync the payload first and the header second. A replacement selects the older slot when no slot must be carried. An incomplete sidecar without a slot is initialized afresh. Bytes after both slots' payloads can be truncated. Listing slots verifies headers and orders them newest first. A reader also checks the payload digest before a slot becomes visible. Recovery scans footer candidates backward to choose the newest root whose slot matches and verifies, or a finished root with no slot, then truncates the recorded file after the recovered footer. Resume takes the slot sequence from that root, allowing the next write to overwrite an uncommitted slot without touching the recovered one.

The footer scan overlaps search blocks by the magic length minus one. All candidate root hashes share a budget of twice the file's length, so a long tail of footer-shaped bytes has bounded cost. An unexpected short read caused by a file changing underneath a reader is an I/O error. Zstd frames declare their decoded size. Decompression grows in 128 KiB steps as data arrives, bounded by the size declaration. The root stores the compression level only if all its compressed extents agree and the level is known. Older roots lacking the optional trailing field decode it as absent, and resuming at another level clears the claim.

Each root orders sections by their kind and id. World-log, records, and facts sections are singletons with id zero. The world log holds one directory per run in run order, records hold opaque campaign controls, and facts carry packs oldest first. Family answers and final states use the family id. Catalogues hold ordered id maps. Their chunks give the first id and count followed by length-prefixed keys. The next chunk starts at the next unused id. A map restore point carries its hour, ordinal, and owner-chosen cadence in its extent tags. It can be stored using its owner's codec or one zstd frame and the restore writer continues at the next ordinal when reopened.

Frozen section extents are appended once. Each level holds fewer than 32 entries, and a full group becomes one index page on the level above. Higher levels come first when locating a frozen position. The format's maximum of twelve levels bounds a section to at most 2^60 extents. An index verifies each entry's level, owner, monotone byte order, and end within the committed length. The number of reachable extents is bounded by the recording's committed bytes. Open extents follow the frozen ones and the next revision may replace them. The container leaves the tags of opaque data to the owning section. `put_from` copies an extent through a bounded buffer, and an appended extent stays unnamed until the writer adds it to a section.
