# Derive spending from covered publications

The power ledger attributes a payment to the canonical slot of its raw country at the hour the payment was read. An unresolved link keeps the explicit no-holder identity. Items are identified by typed operands, assigned an ordinal at their first payment and disambiguated when their length-delimited hashes collide. A charge distinguishes an unevaluated subtraction from a change that is not a payment. Daily upkeep, costs and deficits are merged by the native day. The totals preserve payment counts, balance changes and credits separately.

The first hour of a run exposes the existing game backlog. Its power changes and cost publications do not count toward spending or holds. Totals and open-day parts reflect the input coverage. If a required input is partial or missing, its named status propagates and the affected totals are withheld. Coverage is taken from the last linked raw country with a capture and is published per currency and hour. A payment requires a witness covering its epoch, event hour and currency at the read hour. A daily cost also needs a covered daily cost stream and a batch that reconciles with the recorded political application. A query uses an open part only when its currency has been covered since the day began.

`CountryPowerDaily` holds the latest daily application, so a replaced or deleted row closes the previous day using its retained cells. The latest observed daily costs of an open day are grouped by holder, application and item. A complete republication of today's batch replaces it: an open-day part of the same day and application that the latest batch no longer holds is withdrawn, while parts from power changes stay. A batch that only lost rows acts on the parts its application already holds, grouped as they were published: a part its remaining rows still pay exactly is kept, one they pay differently is rebuilt from them, and one they no longer pay is withdrawn. The application's remaining parts keep their coverage, and its holder counts as reconciled, only when a daily application read that hour matches the remaining sum. A country with no cost rows withdraws its batch only when a covered daily application read that hour records a zero cost; otherwise the missing rows are missing evidence and withdraw nothing. Parts do not name their raw country, so for a holder shared by several raw countries a part a deletion changes or drops is kept with an unknown amount and no coverage rather than rebuilt or withdrawn. The no-holder identity gathers every unlinked country and is never reconciled. A country whose link moves from one holder to another mid-day takes its batch with it. When the cost rows are read that hour, the old holder's parts of an application only the moving country lists are withdrawn and its batch of today is read again under its new holder, if that is a real one. Parts of an application a country that stayed with the old holder lists are kept. Every other part the old holder holds for today, including all of them when the cost rows are not read or the country loses its link, is kept with an unknown amount and no coverage, and nothing is opened under the no-holder identity. After hours the ledger did not run, the first hour with complete cost rows reconciles every open batch before any day settles. Until then a day that closes settles its cost parts as unknown. A batch is the parts of one holder, day and application, and its rows are those its holder's countries, as linked at the last hour the ledger ran, list for that day and application. An unchanged batch is left as it was proven. Otherwise its parts are reconciled like a deletion against the daily application read that hour, which reconciles nothing when the daily rows were not read: exact parts are kept, a changed part is rebuilt, a dropped part is withdrawn, and a new row opens a part, or makes unknown the part another application holds on its ordinal. A rebuilt or new part is covered only when that application reconciles the batch and the capture covers its day. A changed batch of a country that moved, or rows listed by two countries, leave the batch unknown and uncovered. A batch whose rows are gone is withdrawn when a covered daily application of no cost read that hour confirms it, and is unknown and uncovered when that application no longer adds up to its parts or the day is an earlier one; otherwise it is kept for today. A reconciled application is not published again that hour. Batches the ledger never held, including those under the no-holder identity, are not read. An earlier day is settled at once. Positive daily costs count as payments, negative ones as credits, and costs without a matching application on the gains row as unmatched. A batch at or before the run's first hour is omitted. When a value would exceed its stored integer range, it remains unknown until the observation is rebuilt.

Covered command power allocations add a hold. Releases and refunds remove it, and an unattributable refund invalidates all of the holder's holds. Holds are carried while their capture stream remains continuous and reconciled against its allocated total. The recorder rebuilds the ledger indexes from their own tables on resume. The resume blob keeps the last hour, the run's first hour, whether a reconciliation is still due, and each country's link at the last hour.

Table layout is handled in one input module. Power changes come from rows written in the current hour, including available rows from a truncated read. A daily cost publication starts from a country with a cost row written or deleted in that hour, or one that moved holder, and reads its batch in ordinal order, starting from zero.
