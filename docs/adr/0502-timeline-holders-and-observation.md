# Describe timeline holdings at the recorded read

A recorded run covers the whole last hour. A lost interval visibly breaks a bar or line, and its holder remains unknown during that interval. A run can continue across an unrecorded stretch without asserting the holding ended. Join its state wording only when every intervening hour was unobserved and the caller's identity or relation rule agrees. Once another read shows the holding gone, report an end by that read, since the exact end may be somewhere in the gap. Likewise, a holding present after a branch starts or a gap ends can be described as held by that time. Keep native sorted histories linear and use a streaming coverage cursor to handle one long missing span crossing several gaps.

Changing the visible extent during live playback does not move a user's zoom or pan. The final hour's right edge can be drawn as geometry while its label stays attached to the actual read. Point markers do not act as holders. A span's completion mark stays at the reached end, and a live-cut span can leave its trailing edge open within recorded hours. A missing count draws nothing, and a known zero remains represented in the values. If a closed icon-only lane lacks its art, use the holder name to keep it identifiable. A lane configured without art and labels can remain plain.

Stable row IDs bind holders and sub-rows independently of dated names or pictures. A one-entity lane can keep its label when the holder state changes, and its dated art or label can cross-fade under the same identity. Share one vertical value scale across bands and avoid expanding large histories into argument lists. Compute nonoverlapping command sub-rows by inclusive end hour, then reuse those row positions for drawing, pointer resolution and keyboard reveal.
