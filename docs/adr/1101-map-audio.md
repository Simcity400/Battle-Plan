# 1101. Mix presented map audio off the frame thread

Interactive views send audio commands to one process-wide system. Only the current owner can present emitters. An ownership change ends effects and music. A seek, branch change, or perspective change ends effects and lets present loops restart without making old events fire again. A reset is processed in command order. Music plays across seeks and pauses, and an owner can mute between frames. Dumps, still exports, and offline video exports never open an OS audio device; offline video exports with audio enabled drive a private `Engine` synchronously through whole 1,024-frame callbacks (`OfflineMixer`). Silent tracing must be selected before the system starts and advances voices without opening a device.

An emitter's key holds an instance for the life of an entity event. A falling event age means it fired again, and an event first seen more than 100 ms late starts only its loops. Departing one-shots finish, while loops fade out. Interface sounds are unscoped and a later click with the same key can start a new instance. The viewer separates units, combat, and interface volume while defaulting each to the game's effects volume. Camera altitude and the authored ambience and voice categories retain their own volume rules.

The device-free mixer follows native instance order, distance and vertical falloff, stereo balance, polyphony, and `max_audible`. An out-of-range fired start still consumes the sound draw. A created voice consumes the volume draw and an instance outside the audible budget can reclaim a free place when heard again. The weighted draw walks positive weights in order and a uniform fallback may select a sound already playing. Every start draws its delay and rate as required by the definition. Delayed voices enter at their selected volume and an offset fade progresses by a fixed share of full volume over its duration.

Mix 1,024 stereo frames at a time at 44,100 Hz using integer gain steps of 1/16,384. Gain ramps from the previous callback. Rate-shifted voices interpolate and keep the whole-frame cursor between callbacks. Music occupies reserved slots. Among effects, priority times gain selects the first 32, with later slots winning ties. All other voices advance silently. A category compressor runs when a selected voice reaches its bus, including one with zero gain, so its envelope holds otherwise. The master compressor sees the combined mix before clamping. Effects with unreadable samples cannot consume voices indefinitely and named refusals are deduplicated within a content generation.

The control thread feeds a nonblocking whole-frame queue, and the device callback fills any underrun or short final frame with silence. The stream stays on its owning thread. A failure callback latches the first fatal error in an atomic. Reroute, underrun, and real-time priority refusal allow the stream to keep playing. A device that stops consuming is dropped and retried later. Mixing advances by the clock without a device, with bounded work between command reads. Device opens run without holding the status lock. Sound effects decode as whole samples on one loader, and another loader streams music so a track cannot delay effect loading. Stale content completions are discarded and a load failure is reported by name without ending the loader.

At load time, match SDL's conversion to 16-bit stereo at the mixer rate. Files already in that format pass through. The resampler uses silence past the ends of a file, and a device stream carries its padding between mixes. Each put restarts resampling position and retains whole output frames. The SSE2 conversion rounds aligned groups of eight to nearest and truncates scalar samples. The device stream declines channel layouts the converter cannot form. The original SDL attribution remains in the implementation.
