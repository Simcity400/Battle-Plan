# 1403. A raid episode is a run of one key's observations that never contradict, and each of its facts carries the hour it was read

A raid's row key can be reused by a later raid, a load can carry a raid into a new stretch, and hours between reads can be unread. The raid log decides where each raid begins and ends by one rule, not by the key's lifetime.

An episode is a maximal run of observations of one key in which the identity evidence (raider slot, target, raid type) never contradicts and the phase never goes backwards. An observation is a stretch of hours over which the raid's row, its target row and the type's spelling hold; a change of any starts the next one. A complete read without the key, a contradiction of identity, or a lower phase number splits, whether or not the hours between were read. An observation directly after the episode's last one in the same stretch needs only no contradiction. After any hour the key was not observed, inside a stretch as across a load, a break or another run, it joins only when raider, target and type are all known at its own hour and equal; an unreadable target or type is no match. A changed outcome is not a split: the outcome shown is the latest one read.

Every fact of an episode is read from the raid's own rows and carries the hour it was read: its type and icon, its place, its launch, its end (the observed end, then a native end date once read, each with the slot's tag at the end it states), its outcome, its victim (the target's controller at each observation until the first ended one, then a completion country from the hour it is read) and its disappearance (the next whole read without the key, before the key is read again; exact only the hour after its last observation). The raid end log is not read: its rows repeat these reads, and carried by key they belonged to whichever raid held the key.

The reply at an hour is one pure function of the episodes and that hour that keeps only facts read by then (`episode_at` in the product, `raidEpisodeAt` in the client); the full reply is that function at the last hour, so a reply through any hour equals the full reply at that hour, field for field. A reply from an hour clips that history. Artwork, icon warnings and the availability answer come from the episodes and lifetimes up to the requested hour. A tally takes a chosen slot first and otherwise the tag at the event: the slot's tag at launch, and for a success the end tag known at each hour, so a native end date read later moves the success from that hour.
