# 1190. Gather against a held camera and admit against the frame camera

A retained candidate set can use a held camera without using that camera to decide a frame's drawing. A stationary view gathers its exact candidates. A travelling view gathers with eight world units of slack and a turning free eye can also reserve 0.05 of directional chord, about 2.9 degrees. Reuse requires the same viewport, map, height, pose family, lens and view defines. Movement must stay within both allowances. Each frame still tests its actual camera against the gathered candidates. The province schedule also keys the set to the current box generation.

`Camera::turned_from` measures ground travel separately from view rotation. Twice the sine of half the rotation is the maximum change in the direction of a unit vector. Squared differences of the three axes add to twice the squared chord, avoiding the loss of small turns in a trace near three. For a plane through the held eye, the slack at a point is the translation allowance plus the turn allowance times distance. Use the furthest corner of a province's box to bound that distance, and the corner nearest each plane's interior to test exclusion. Both the box tests and the choice of seam views are conservative. An overinclusive gathered set cannot create a drawn province.

`Camera::held_ground` clips a widened volume to the object-height band and includes the held eye, since a covered view looking above the entire band can use its eye for its ground bounds. In `turned_corners`, a bound on the rotation's direction difference gives a worst-case deviation of each side plane and a lower bound on the forward component. If the corner ray's forward cosine is `c` and the allowable chord is `turn`, lean each side normal out by `turn / (c - turn)` times the original forward vector. Use `(c - turn) * near` for the near depth and `(1 + turn * secant) * far` for the far bound. A turn that reaches the corner ray's forward cosine has no such conservative volume, so the hold uses travel alone. Clipping a volume retains the corners in the band and the intersections of the box edges with its boundaries.
