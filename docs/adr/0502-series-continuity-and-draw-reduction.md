# Keep recorded continuity and peaks in chart geometry

Chart runs hold values over inclusive recorded hours. A missing hour or an explicit segment break ends the path and prevents interpolation across that boundary. An adjacent read can start a new segment when a different country has inherited the tag. An exact read through the playhead supersedes summaries across its whole reached range, including hours it reports as missing. Zooming into a run continues its held value from the view's fractional start and stops at the actual end of the recorded hour. End labels follow the last visible portion of each line even if the playhead is in a gap.

Curves sample exact values and preserve an intervening peak or dip. A power-of-two sampling grid anchored at hour zero retains the interior samples during playback and when a window shifts. Changes of sampling resolution reuse the hours shared by both grids. Monotone interpolation keeps each segment within its bounding samples. Shading between lines uses their actual curves and pairs segments only when their breaks match. An exact held-value plot uses steps and shares the same boundaries. Ordered spans are checked and cut with forward cursors so long alternating membership histories scale with their inputs. Cuts carry an explicit break only at its original hour.

Positive and negative bands stack separately. A missing value breaks the stack, and a zero denominator breaks a share plot. A shrinking signed band retains its side until it closes, so it does not cross the axis as it reaches zero. Compensated totals retain small layers beside much larger quantities. Draw reduction preserves the ends of stretches and their visible extremes, including internal shares with a constant total. Compact stacks retain source runs and aggregate bounds, generate edges at drawn hours, and join pieces per complete stretch to avoid seams and a stroke per pixel. Share tiles integrate exact coverage in dense columns and reuse pixels as the playhead moves. Data updates invalidate the changed suffix. A followed tile or summary can request a repaint as it lands, and the live head clips the last bin to the reached hour.

Bar strips reveal a bar only when its recording read has been reached. A shared pixel column keeps the largest visible amount for each half and the identity of its source bar, with later bars winning ties. Pointer hit testing uses the same rectangles and painter order, with a column fallback that keeps short bars reachable. A keyboard stop and a pointer target can describe the hour of the same read while their marker remains inside a clipped view. Two reads can share a screen position and remain distinct keyboard stops. Bucketed summaries describe intervals with unknown individual read hours, so they supply no exact bar stop.

Calendar ticks derive from the recorded hours. Pick the finest calendar level with room for its labels and measure the visible portion of each period. The first visible month or quarter label includes the year, and January carries it at later year boundaries. Quarter labels use the first month's tick. Dates are left empty outside the supported calendar.
