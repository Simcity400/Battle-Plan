# 0308. Separate observed political history from content presentation

The idea timeline folds changes in active ideas, advisors and their character and lexical rows per country holder. Both active-idea and advisor histories must be observed to continue a span. Identity starts establish the held ideas without a change event. A change of advisor, seat or character presentation can split a span, while a rename or newly resolved token alone gives no evidence of an appointment. Recorded modifier definitions are shared across formatting requests for the source and timeline. Readers process identity-start hours in order instead of opening the same tables at every hour.

The recorded advisor seat takes precedence over the content's role or group. The character template at the latest holder's relevant span decides between conflicting authored roles. Mod-defined categories and their order are preserved. The character's recorded saved name takes precedence over the original authored name. A missing script token prevents a portrait from being attributed to an advisor's template. Each known holder carries its own portrait and name. An undefined trait or a missing file cannot establish that an effect list is empty. Hidden modifiers stay out of display, and command fields combine with the advisor's own cost.

The ruling-party history follows recorded holders over consecutive observed hours. It connects ideology tokens to recorded party indices for popularity tiles and uses authored colors or the palette when content is unavailable. A change of holder or a recording gap ends the evidence for a change of government. An election already recorded on the first observation supplies no new event. A new last-election value on a later read for the same holder dates the observation, including after a gap. The party view only renders names that can resolve without unavailable runtime scope. Histories without explicit sample-hour metadata use the table's available hours to carry its last read.
