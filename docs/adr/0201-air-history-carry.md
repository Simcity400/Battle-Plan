# 0201. Carry air patrol history only after a complete hour

Eight acquisition parts own native country slots by slot modulo part count, sharing the same hourly opening certificate from AirMissions. Each part operates on its own staging and certificates. The host owns dispatch, joins every part before settling the hour, and loans the frame and fingerprints to decoding. The spool grows staging for observed demand with headroom. A larger legitimate read is allowed to grow in its worker instead of withholding the hour at a prior peak.

An opening certifies quiet only after every part from the preceding hour completed and its frame was kept. A drop, invalidation or acquisition failure revokes the opening so the next hour reads every country. Refused workers stay refused, without running a substitute read on the game thread. A reused frame starts only after its previous staging was decoded or dropped. Decode applies completed copies in hour order, then publishes the selected countries in country, region and native summation order. A mismatch against held history poisons the held copy and withholds its publication until a complete fresh hour decodes.

The watch domain observes the air object and each allocated regional history. It registers receivers before reading and accepts revision, receiver and site certificates only within the same epoch while no writer runs. A quiet site can justify omitting a read only for a watched receiver. An unobserved receiver is copied again. Slot identities are checked on every walk. A removed slot loses its certificate, leaving its future occupant to be verified afresh. Notifications use the registered receiver identities. Disabled observation, poison, refused registration and load, gap or recovery all force fresh reads where certainty was lost.

The add observer marks the country air object and a separate key for its region. That key uses bits outside a user object address. A region-key collision merely causes another read. Adds affect the daily current entry, so a changed add key with no advance permits a read of that entry alone. If a full menu copy is unavailable, the copy uses the daily ring. A refused current-record patch retries a whole copy. An advance-site receiver bumps its sequence on entry, and certificates are sampled with no writer running. Each completed advance moves the ring one slot, wraps if needed and zeroes the newly current record. A same-epoch sequence delta therefore advances an unmodified full or daily history without reading its channels. An add and an advance together can change closed records and require a whole copy.

The registry retains watches until invalidation. The spool offers a replacement sized for the country roster, one history per known strategic region and receivers already registered. The game thread installs the table at the hourly boundary. Retirement and freeing stay off that thread. Sizing ahead prevents the first visit to each known region from clearing the table and forcing a full re-read. Diagnostic restart counts distinguish invalidation, watch growth and incomplete preceding hours.

The history observer's `Hooks::drop` releases its watch registry after the last capture, with the hooks either unpublished or restored and drained. The same quiescence protects the relays and the registries accessed by their callbacks.
