# Post processing uses fixed reduction targets and recorded grading

**Status:** Accepted

The viewer grades the resolved HDR scene from the recording's post-effect volumes and graphics defines. Each named LUT is resolved once in first-seen order, with an identity cube for an empty path. The cube is 32 slices across a 1024 by 32 texture, with a nearest-byte RGB lattice. Upload releases the decoded images and retains the volume data. A recorded policy that covers the volume file verifies it. Policy gaps and artwork that is outside that coverage are reported through the layer reader. Malformed operands refuse the pass. If a visual mod's replacement effect cannot be drawn, the viewer retries the recorded effect and reports the replacement's refusal.

The seed halves each HDR dimension by truncating division, including when the resolved scene was itself halved. It puts the bright pass and emissive contribution in RGB and log luminance in alpha. Luminance reduction reuses two fixed single-channel half-float targets with shrinking ceil-half viewports. On odd sizes the native UV arithmetic can sample outside the previous active rectangle, so a mip chain or clamping to the active rectangle would change the result. The final gather reads the last rectangle and the previous exposure. Full-size restore linearly samples the resolved scene before tone mapping and grading. GUI overlays draw over the full-size output. Disabling post effects directly copies the scene into that output, avoiding reuse of the previous frame and leaving unused exposure history alone.

Exposure uses two single-texel targets. A gather writes the opposite parity from the one it reads, and restore samples the written result. An output-size or world-size change starts fresh exposure. Hiding a view keeps exposure that has been used. A fresh history or stationary replay dump adapts instantly so the dump stands alone. Playing exposure scales the authored adaptation speed by frame time. Bloom uses separable filters with tap spacing in seed texels. Gaussian tap pairs fold into linear samples and a zero-weight bloom binds a zeroed texel and records no blur commands. This bloom is independent of the map's separate bloom display switch.

Grading chooses the highest faded-in height volume and the first box that contains the eye. With no height volume, it skips boxes. Night strength applies before saturation. Volume blending preserves the order of day and night, height, winter and box-edge operations. Within a double blend both mixes are performed in one draw, while results between draws are stored and rounded as UNORM8. Source LUTs and intermediate targets stay distinct. Endpoints and identical or unchanged grades skip unnecessary draws. The selected grade is reused when only the night factor changes by less than 0.001. HSV and colour balance use neutral renderer constants.

Winter grading uses raw recorded provincial snow, separate from the display snow and mud. Each box builds its set of representatives from the map geometry once per session. It retains one per strategic region after rejecting duplicate, non-land and non-overlapping provinces. Authoring order chooses the last overlapping land province, and the whole area of each overlapping province counts even when it mostly lies outside the box. An edge touch alone does not count. The weight spans the complete region contribution. Without both winter definitions and nonzero snow the box uses its summer values. A missing representative reading with both winter variants withholds the box's contribution and retains the height grade. Prepared hours can compute winter factors without advancing the current grade or exposure.

The post passes retain each allocation and binding until target, history, LUT source or blur admission changes. Each pass has a device-aligned block in one retained buffer. Changed operands update the relevant blocks in place. Bindings whose exposure views depend on parity retain both variants. The seed and restore share their scene-grade uniform, with full output dimensions in the viewport terms even if the scene is smaller. The authored raster and binding sequence remains significant. Reductions retain seed state, restore retains the seed's uniform, and unsupported reflected reads refuse instead of guessing previous state. Shader validation tracks initialized uniform ranges and preserves unknown tails. The LUT pass draws into colour-only targets and retains alpha when the authored program only writes RGB.
