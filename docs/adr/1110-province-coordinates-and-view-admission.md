# 1110. Keep province geometry, view admission and picking in their own coordinate contracts

Decoded province and height images count rows from the north. World z counts from the south, so the pixel conversion reverses the row. The ordinary province lookup truncates toward zero before checking bounds, allowing a negative subpixel coordinate to name zero. It returns no province for an unclaimed or off-map pixel. Callers that explicitly address a raster column across the date line use the wrapping accessor.

Province boxes grow from painted bitmap runs, rounded outward to even coordinates. An unpainted province has no box. Admission shares these boxes across province scheduling and scene consumers. A box survives plane contact. For each camera view, the box corner nearest a plane's interior determines whether the entire box lies outside it. Height participates in that test, so a building can remain visible when its ground point is outside. The orbit tests four side planes with a margin. The free camera adds near and far planes bounded by its object range. Held admission expands the planes and seam reach to cover bounded travel and supported free-eye turns. Actual frame admission still uses its own planes. [Held camera admission](1190-held-camera-admission.md)

Orbit seam reach uses normalized corner rays to the water plane, capped at five eye heights. The signs come from the original footprint. A footprint entirely past a seam recenters by one map width per frame, and a straddling footprint retains its center. The renderer uses canonical geometry and shifts the view, with the east copy before the west. A free camera brings its main view onto the first copy and adds all other overlapping copies within its reach, nearest first and east first on ties.

Province centroids use truncated means of integer raster positions. The sums follow wrapping signed arithmetic. Directed borders retain each owned pixel once per distinct neighbor and wrap in x. Border ordering starts with the least-connected pixel and grows from the nearer end, preserving the traversal's first-tie and swap-removal order. For a centroid outside its own province, the nearest owned pixel search visits runs by world z and then x and keeps the first pixel at an equal Manhattan distance. Movement-map content supplies neighbor order, crossing and kind.

Map consumers use different height samples to match the surface they place on. Border rails and route splines use clamped, bilinear height samples after removing the half-pixel shift, with the sea plane as a floor. The rendered surface follows the terrain index pattern. Each cell's parity picks its diagonal, x wraps and z clamps. The camera and walkers use that triangular surface. Raid arrows interpolate the four surrounding fixed-point samples in x then z, with their own wrap and clamp rules and without an offset or sea floor. The nearest-sample helper for test consumers wraps x and clamps the polar row.

Orbit province picking searches nearest-sampled height cells in quadrant order with the orbit's seam views. The picking walk retains the native 256-candidate capacity across those views. Candidate distance uses the cell's upper corner offset by half a pixel, with y set to the sampled height. It falls back to the y=0 plane and then the zero vector when no view reaches it. Wheel-centered camera motion reads only the camera's own view and can use a plane hit on any copy, so moving over the wrap does not jump. The free camera walks cells nearest first to its far depth and returns no hit above the horizon. Province selection wraps a free-camera hit to the first copy, while free-camera motion keeps it on the ray's own copy.
