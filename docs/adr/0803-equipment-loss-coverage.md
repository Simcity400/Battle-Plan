# Keep loss totals and evidence of coverage together

Equipment loss inputs name raw countries. Resolve each through the recorded country links and count the loss for each current identity of the canonical slot. A Combat Log entry repeated under aliases contributes once, using the highest alias. The writer needs both country identity tables to run. After a gap, it rereads its held inputs and limits an alias or identity recount to the slots affected. A recount sums each affected leaf from the held and dropped tables without copying every log row.

Each complete `EquipmentDestruction` read contributes the hour's drain even when all its cells match the previous read. A held table without the current read stamp supplies no drain. Startup and branch transition intervals are encoded with the zero-key `EquipmentDestructionCoverage::PartialInterval` marker (while recognizing the frozen legacy unavailable reason string only for recordings prior to `EQUIPMENT_DESTRUCTION_STARTUP_FORMAT`) and recorded into `EquipmentLossCoverage.drain_state` alongside the display text. A partial interval before the first complete drain establishes no coverage and never withholds the run. After counting starts, partial intervals, missed reads, unresolved owners or missing supplemental writers leave a lower bound, preserving `DRAIN_STATE_LOST` and its explanation alongside any co-occurring drain state on the same hour. A refusal, truncation, or unavailable drain withholds those leaves for the remainder of the run. Combat Log land coverage separately checks the captured DLC operand, eligibility inputs and whether the relevant divisions and prune were observed.

Keep the last amount of a dropped Combat Log key until it returns or the game prunes it permanently. Within an hour use the last deletion of a key. Complete `CombatLogMonths` supplies the oldest surviving entry per array and permits immediate freeing when the line and its month disappear together. Otherwise use the recorded retention to free entries only after expiry is certain. A line moved out of the dropped table contributes to its leaf's freed amount so the total survives a prune and a resume. A fresh identity receives the freed amount of the other identities of its canonical slot.

The ship loss log writes signatures whose count increased in the latest complete sunk-ship read or whose slot must be recounted. Each row carries the cumulative count of that signature, the sinking hour, and a second hash to distinguish signatures sharing the compact key. The reply follows the highest count seen for a signature across the relevant rows. After an uncomparable read, log every listed signature. A falling convoy count starts a new count. A deleted lend-lease pool can leave a lower bound from after its preceding read. The resume blob holds the live convoy and lend-lease comparisons and read hours. The other state is restored from the tables.

The journal parity test runs a table read with identical cells as well as an hour without a read stamp. Memory-label tests run alone because their allocation labels and phases belong to the process.

`NavalLossTotals` is a different view. Its first complete read of each kind establishes a baseline rather than new losses. Sunk ship entries have no stable identity and can move in the list, so compare full-entry multiplicity with a stable hash and an independent check. Convoy losses come from signed changes to the recorded monthly killer-owner buckets. Counts are attributed to each resolving country identity for both relevant sides. Incomplete hours remain unknown. A later read can continue from retained entries, but a gap spanning the recorded retention, or an unavailable retention value, makes the later totals lower bounds. Use years of hours for ship retention and calendar months for convoy retention. Restore the comparison inputs and identities from the committed tables and keep the missing intervals in the writer blob.
