# Preserve capture order with one FIFO decode handoff

The game thread fills a decode job in its frame slot so it does not move the job through its stack. Every frame goes through the same FIFO, including frames with no refresh payload. The frame-linked queue waits for no capacity and lets a backlog use more frames. This keeps handoff order independent of decode time or the frame's diagnostic sequence label. The consumer owns the slot from the pop through the ready handoff and leaves no frame reference or context lease live at publication. A dropped unpublished reservation clears its slot field by field.

Each staged payload freezes per-table activity and owns its copied bytes. Decode must honor that activity even when the envelope would request a different cadence or a forced refresh. A refresh flag names a promised payload. An undeclared payload can mean acquisition already installed typed results. A declared missing payload, a decode panic, or a refused raw-log index has an explicit table-specific failure. Typed fallbacks are decided during acquisition. Accepted decode has no retry of live memory. A refused contained allocation costs only its family's tables. Optional failures leave inactive tables and other owned results alone.

The cadence table is authoritative. A family enters staging if any of its rows are due. Shared paths still account acquisition time once, charging the groups slot for army upgrades, land theatres and orders groups for their shared roots, deployment conveyors and licences for their walks, building construction and occupation settings for theirs, and the hub, definitions, fuel and weather regions slots for their shared inputs. The bases, registrations and aces share one acquisition log across the air and fog packs. The per-frame table timings measure the copies only. Decode has separate calibration measurements and charges the whole grown frame before handing it to the spool.

The worker drains retired staging payloads after taking each frame and before publishing it. The callback has then retired that frame's rejected payloads, and no reuse of the frame can add a new retirement to that drain. An incomplete frame returns its held-store loans before publication so subsequent capture cannot reuse partial results. The presentation loan has no raw decode dependency and returns before unrelated work can stall it.

Retirement transfers storage on the game thread and wakes an idle worker even when no frame was enqueued. A contended queue or refused growth releases that payload locally rather than making the callback wait. The worker prepares space for every in-flight frame, grows and clears retired payloads, and keeps one per held frame beyond the steady pool. As the backlog clears, retention returns to the steady size. A staging miss can borrow an empty payload and size it on the worker. A whole-frame allocation refusal follows the existing loss path. Backlog memory must account for retained arenas as well as typed frame storage.

The loader prepares queues and pools before callbacks can reach them, maintains the worker watchdog and releases ownership only after quiescence. Failed detach retains anything reachable from a callback. A competing unpublished initializer may drop only its own allocation. Normal detach stops and joins decode, then the loader drains any remaining jobs synchronously with a fresh memo while the spool still consumes ready frames. The worker or that quiescent loader is the only retirement consumer. Detach drains decode before the spool so queued hours keep their order.
