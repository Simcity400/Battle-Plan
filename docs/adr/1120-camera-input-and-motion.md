# Drive viewer cameras from frame input and drawn terrain

Camera input uses elapsed wall time, with a cap on one frame's integration so a stall cannot produce a large jump. Sticks keep direction through a radial dead zone, saturate before the rim and use a curved response for small corrections. Exponential easing gives the same response across frame rates. Movement keys use their physical positions. This keeps the layout consistent and releases a key such as Plus even after a modifier changes its printed meaning. The viewer takes the pointer for free movement and forgets held keys on Escape or loss of focus.

The desktop host chooses the controller owner from the foreground window that receives device input. A map accepting a handoff inherits held buttons without generating presses. Only a host release clears its controller. A stale state or a different handoff cannot affect it, and a button held through a transfer cannot double-press. Real edges from between frames still count. The state distinguishes stick coordinates with positive up from captured pointer motion with positive down. Both devices track double taps and allow an already-consumed press to clear the pending tap.

Orbit preserves the ground target and camera height. Pan speed scales with height to provide similar screen motion at different zooms, and both keyboard and stick pan relative to the rotated view. Drag and wheel preserve the point under the pointer. The default heading and pitch give the game's original camera, even at heights where that pitch lies past the orbit range. Reset follows the shorter turning arc and cancels any remaining turn and tilt rate. East and west wrap, and the ground target stays inside the map's rows.

Flight is an arcade camera. It maintains a minimum airspeed, preserves the pilot's throttle and clamps lead, pitch and bank. A long mouse motion cannot wrap the heading lead into a turn the other way. World positions use map units and headings increase clockwise from north. Chase view draws the aircraft and scales its offset with the model's radius. Nose view follows its attitude and omits the aircraft. While a crash plays, both views watch from the last chase pose. Aircraft meshes face their local negative Z, with local X pointing to their left, so the native basis uses left, up and backward. Each respawn gets a new animation identity.

Flight collision sweeps the origin, nose, tail and wingtips before wrapping the position. Ground and water are piecewise planar along the cell edges and diagonals. Checking segment endpoints and each crossing gives the exact test against the higher surface and the content's contact offset. One frame is subdivided by elapsed time and distance. The explosion stays until the entity's own clip reaches the authored delay or no such clip can play. Respawn waits for both the explosion and the minimum delay. It tries a clear level start over the wreck on the same heading, using a conservative maximum of the ground inside its forward footprint. When that does not fit the ceiling, a cached row and column maximum search finds the closest clear map vertex from the wreck across the seam, favouring the heading on an exact tie. A map with no clear vertex keeps the wreck.

The walking figure uses body heights for movement, jumping, falling, camera distance and near clearance. This keeps controls in proportion when its scale changes. Walk defaults to first person (eye at 0.92 body heights, ±85° pitch, figure omitted) and switches between first and third person on V or the controller's North button. The minimum scale spans one step of the height map and the maximum fits the figure under the content ceiling; in third person the camera distance shrinks to stay under the ceiling. Feet stand on the drawn triangles or the water above them. Pressing Space or South jumps from the ground with constant airtime across scales; double tapping toggles flight (including mid-jump). Flight uses a longer ease for a smooth creative-mode glide, holds altitude unless climbing or descending, and lands on downward contact. The Fly free camera reuses the same flying motion locked in first person with no body, gravity or landing, using the scale multiplier (via `=`/`-`, D-pad or scroll wheel, reset on `0` or West) as speed. In third person the figure turns into its ground motion and uses its walking animation and scroll flags only while walking. The eye stays on the map and follows the figure at the vertical edges, and its horizontal wrapping preserves the look direction. A new content instance can choose the figure again without moving the walker. A placed dump walk integrates the capture clock, while interactive walking uses frame input.
