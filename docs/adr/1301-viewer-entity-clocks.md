# 1301. Keep actor clocks and animation events in presentation state

Presentation owns its seed and entity history. The recording does not contain the native renderer's random stream or state-entry clock. A stable actor handle supplies the seed, while the full actor address identifies its events and retained samples. Ambient entities, buildings, trains and named raid stages use authored state and clip choices. The train's authored idle state is its moving clip. A fresh raid actor goes straight to its state. An established actor may play the intro and let its authored successor take over. A missing request leaves the actor unchanged, and asking for the current state does not finish active events.

Use a sole variant regardless of its weight. For several variants, draw with positive authored weights and use a uniform draw if all are zero. Advance across explicit callback times before sampling a frame. Ordinary callbacks skip the current state. Naval callbacks can reenter it and retain the phase of played clips while hidden traversal suppresses automatic successors and state events. Hidden attached nodes without a played animation stop traversing. Played clips still advance through the animation manager. The first naval request carries the normal-state range. A state without animation can change entity events while leaving the current mesh clip in place.

Visit attached children in the native order. Apply state transitions to descendants before producing their event transforms. Joint event positions include baked mesh scale, but the basis passed to particles retains only entity scale. A locator with no matching joint stays in entity space, including on a meshless parent. Native draw and event packets use source-order actors. Cached actor poses require the same rig and equal relevant samples, and the samples can be evaluated away from the player. Despawn discards its player and history.

Effects carry elapsed entity age separately from clip phase. Hidden actors can suppress effect drawing while retaining elapsed particle and light simulation. Leaving a state preserves any kept emitters until their particle runtime says they have finished, even after the automatic successor begins. Train exhaust can therefore outlive its departure state. Aircraft retains the full route and rig basis, including roll.

Owners that retire an entity at the end of its played root clip read its play position and the clip's length. When it has completed, that owner reads the old clip's length even if the successor has started. Nonlooping timing reaches completion in animation seconds, and an animationless root can still hold the previous played clip. These rules let a launch or crash determine its exact retirement boundary without relying on the actor's total age.
