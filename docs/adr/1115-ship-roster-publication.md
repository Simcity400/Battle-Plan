# 1115. Resolve ship rosters separately from frame placement

The ships layer keeps task forces by recording slot and orders them by task-force id, matching direct model selection. It places the visible figures on the frame and resolves their hulls on a worker over decoded rows. The camera warms rosters before the task force enters the picture, using its height as the world-space margin around the viewport so the margin scales with the view. Moving the camera within one side of the model cutoff leaves the declaration intact. A task force in battle or competing with a land occupant needs the full model builder instead. Vision changes invalidate only affected task forces unless the viewing perspective or a whole sight input changes.

A roster answer must come from the current seek and from rows at or after its request hour. A backward seek can otherwise reuse a result for a later hour and hide or draw the wrong ships indefinitely. The layer matches answers by the mirror slot, allowing the worker to answer a newly born task force before the frame has read its row. A stale roster is requested again, and a task force that leaves the roster set is forgotten so a later refresh cannot rebuild it needlessly. The worker compares dated graphics-pool decisions even when only the hour changes, because a mod can change a roster choice without changing any input row other than the clock. The worker also learns additional tables that its content scripts read and does not re-evaluate against an old frame missing those inputs.

The visible product stands at the newest hour all admitted figures can support. A pending roster bounds it by both the hour of the answered rows and the previous hour of the change that caused the pending request. Work asked before a figure arrived cannot make it older than the first hour the camera admitted it. Off-screen rosters cannot delay the picture. Worker requests count toward publication age, while frame placement marks count toward carried work. Hidden and refused layers draw nothing and carry nothing. A whole settle waits for rosters of the applied hour and names a refusal or timeout.

Regression tests step the head to a named hour and synchronize on roster worker handovers. This covers slot reuse, backward seeks, camera admission, and a second roster request outstanding after the first finishes. A stepper's newest buffered hour varies with scheduling and is unsuitable for an assertion about a particular hour.
