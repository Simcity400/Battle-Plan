# 0201. Keep slice selection bound to complete recording evidence

Each sliced family chooses one owner identity domain. Native country IDs, canonical slots that follow tag aliases, and native state IDs are independent. A frame holds its own selection, and the attachment keeps a separate fingerprint store for each family. The spool merges the registered family's tables by owner and stamps the owners actually read, including those whose complete read produced zero rows. An owner the census does not see is removed only after a complete census, so a failed or truncated scan cannot remove a still-live owner. Reappearing IDs then count as new.

A rotating slice covers one portion of owners per hour. New owners, changed or unreadable fingerprints, exceptional selections and hooked-writer marks can bring a read forward. Equal fingerprints alone do not skip a rotating slice. New, stale and unreadable observations never justify carry. A full hour reads every owner. A daily family instead refreshes at the first hour after the daily tick plus its marked owners between ticks. A fully watched family can omit that boundary too, reading new, explicitly selected or marked owners only. A change-driven family can trust an unmarked owner only if the take has the same epoch and global inputs as its last complete read. A failed take, changed global hash or moved epoch requires a fresh full read.

An unpublished selection invalidates each early owner and any affected slice. If a take spent its marks, the next take cannot certify the missing interval, so a fresh read is required. Observed owner lists grow from the IDs actually encountered rather than preallocating the numeric domain. They stay ordered for stable output and also accept sparse IDs. Allocation refusal makes the pass incomplete.

Research and stability add a daily drift read to writer-marked owners. Technology rosters use a constant fingerprint for new or unpublished owners and share their definitions' read cadence. A settled owner can skip its slice when its fingerprint incorporates the drift state. Lazy drift inspection then reads only owners already selected or lacking a valid held print, or whose slice or daily boundary is due. The held research stores keep each country's last complete rosters and corresponding slots. They reread banked time and assigned-technology progress in roster order and place the rows in slot-index order. Duplicate assignments share the one technology's progress read. The original serial walk still determines the reported refusal when a batched gather faults. A successful serial fallback may publish its own complete values. Held allocation is advisory, so only a complete clone enters the held store.

Other families choose the policy that matches their readers. Relations and available advisor pools can wait for their daily slice. Combat Log preselects countries whose retired-order logs are about to be pruned. Supply train inputs are decorative and normally read at the slice. Air relations keep their fingerprint store on the decode side. Naval mission fingerprints cover every recorded field, so changed ones act as marks. Raid launch is fully watched while state inputs retain a daily slice for origin states with an unobserved supply-route flag. Focus and projects refresh every owner after the daily tick and follow writer marks between ticks. Project rows use the stored project owner. Trade routes follow recorded holder countries, and changes in their referenced-route set prompt an immediate read.

The FNV-1a fingerprint stays in process. Its zero-suffix multiplication preserves the bytewise hash, and length-prefixed text keeps distinct adjacent string sequences from aliasing by concatenation.
