# Preserve the map camera and give the free eye a separate projection contract

The map camera is a left-handed look-at camera. Its centre is on the water plane, and the eye stands behind it by a fixed horizontal lead derived from camera tilt. Height changes zoom without changing the lead. A free eye instead carries an independent position, yaw, pitch, roll and lens. Its yaw is clockwise from north, positive pitch looks above the horizon, and positive roll lowers the right side. Clip pitch short of vertical so heading remains meaningful. Runtime content supplies camera and graphics operands and the user folder supplies preferences. Shipped values are synthetic fixtures. Ambient and diffuse colours are linearized from authored values. The map bitmap supplies world dimensions and integer wrap width.

Keep row-vector view-projection matrices in CPU memory. Writing their rows directly as WGSL columns gives the transposed column-vector transform, with matching normalized depth conventions. Compute the cofactor inverse in double precision because large world translations share the matrix with near-unit projection terms. Pick rays read exact fractional NDC when shadow footprints require it, derive the inverse terms directly from the lens, and bypass the eye-point round trip for free eyes to avoid losing direction near large world coordinates. Map-icon distance cutoffs divide the fixed-point operand after converting to single precision, which can differ from rounding after the division.

Use a single nearest projected X copy for ordinary wrapped anchors. Callers projecting a direction between two anchors use one common copy. The dedicated map-icon projection first moves anchors within half a world width of the eye and uses the integer GUI extent. It keeps signed perspective division even behind the map camera, matching its callers' conversion. For a free eye reject points on or behind the eye plane and position any obligatorily retained anchor outside the viewport at greatest depth. Free-camera icon cutoffs use distance to the nearest world copy, and the map camera uses height. Keep world text quads aligned to the camera axes.

The ordinary water-plane footprint cannot reach the horizon at the map camera's supported pose and height. The free eye can look above it, so cut the convex frustum by its object distance and clip to the terrain and object height band before taking a horizontal footprint. Empty clipped geometry has no extent beyond the allowed margin around the eye. Frustum planes use nonnegative inside tests and depth from zero to one. The free eye wraps horizontally, clamps within the map's vertical rows, and stays between terrain clearance and the upper camera height. The ordinary map camera instead clamps the footprint to the map plus recorded north and south allowances. Centre overshoot when the visible footprint cannot fit. Explicit headless camera placement stays unchanged. Fit-to-map bisects height because the fixed lead makes the footprint nonlinear, then stops at the recorded maximum if the map cannot fit.

Camera admission pads a moving view to keep an already admitted set across a few frames of compatible pan, then admits the exact view when motion stops. Padding may send extra offscreen draws to the GPU. Movement that changes the view shape cannot reuse that admission. Anchored overlays have a separate layout pad and displacement contract.

For ordinary interaction, gather edge pan, per-notch wheel movement and arrow pan in that order. Edge pan uses the cursor's whole pixel and respects the corner-scrolling preference. Project each pan through the camera's right and up plane without normalizing, so aspect and diagonal input affect its size. Preserve fractional wheel lines across turns and process whole notches separately, since a blocked notch cannot cancel a later valid one. Wheel movement follows the ground ray with the height-dependent forward correction and zoom scale, caps input frame time, and dampens sideways travel when the floor truncates a step. The floor comes from terrain under the shown eye and the configured minimum. The minimap wheel changes target height around its look-at point. Keyboard zoom starts from shown height, and a clamped move shorter than one unit is dropped. Viewer drag moves the shown camera and its target directly by translating the ground point under the cursor.

Ease the shown camera toward the persistent target with the configured interpolation and camera-speed setting, clamped to a full step per frame. Keep the target after settling so the next wheel notch starts there. Clamp target and shown heights to the whole-unit limits, and carry the target's vertical coordinate with a map-boundary correction so easing does not repeatedly chase an unreachable point. When the user settings file is absent use the constructed preferences.
